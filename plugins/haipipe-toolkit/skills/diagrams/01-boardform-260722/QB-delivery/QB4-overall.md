## Opening
How can pages of different kinds feel familiar without forcing them to carry the same content?
Pages here do different jobs.
One settles a decision, one carries a stage of a paper, one is generated from a skill folder, and more kinds will come.
What they hold has almost nothing in common, so it is tempting to give each kind its own layout.
This page does the opposite: every kind opens in the same order, and only Content changes shape.

**What a page kind is**: A family of pages that hold the same sort of material.
The board adds a new one whenever it meets work the existing kinds do not fit, so the list is open and this page never enumerates it.

**Where this page sits**: The board works down one ladder: Board, Group, Page, Section, Sentence.
QB2 takes the Index, where a reader chooses which page to open, and QB3 puts that page's file beside the work it describes.
This page is the next rung down, and it is the one everyone stands on: the page itself, what a writer opens to edit and a reader opens to read.
QB5 carries the ladder on into the section and the sentence, so this page stops at the whole page and the order its sections keep.

**What one grammar buys**: A reader who has opened any page can open the next without being taught again, because the order never changes.
Leaving Content free to change shape is what lets a kind carry the material it exists to show without buying a new page layout along with it.

**What the alternative costs**: If a kind rearranged the page around its own substance, every handoff would begin with navigation rather than discussion.

## Writing Style
How this page must be written. Read it before editing, and edit to it.

**Title**: The title says what the page is FOR, not merely which topic it covers (JL 260801).
Write it as a phrase in sentence case, capitalizing the first word and proper nouns and nothing else.
A colon may follow the phrase with a short subtitle, and that subtitle is usually where the purpose actually lands.
`Page Template design` named a topic in two conflicting cases; `The page template: one grammar every page kind obeys` names the same subject and tells the reader what it obliges them to do.

**Everything after the first blank line**: It renders in the `More details` fold, and it is written as a list of labelled parts, never one block of prose (JL 260801).
Start each part with a bold label saying what it answers, then its sentences, with a blank line between parts.
The paragraph on stage is read straight through; `More details` is opened by someone hunting one specific thing, and a block of prose gives them no label to scan for.

**Every heading passes the weak-English test**: A reader who does not read English well must be able to tell what a paragraph is about from its heading alone (JL 260801).
Prefer short common words to precise rare ones, and name the consequence rather than the mechanism.

✅ `A blank line decides what people see`

❌ `The opening paragraph ends at the first blank line`

**A good/bad pair gets its own line**: Mark it `✅ the good one  ❌ the bad one`, on a line of its own, never buried in a sentence (JL 260801).
A contrast written as prose makes the reader hold four quoted phrases at once and work out which two are the good ones, which is exactly the work an example exists to save.

**Language and sentences**: English only, in the source and in the render (JL 260724).
Write one sentence per line, so a paragraph is consecutive lines rather than one long line.
That keeps diffs readable and lets a single sentence be quoted, commented on, and addressed on its own.
Never build a long noodle sentence that hangs three or four clauses off one colon; break it into short sentences that each carry a single idea.
No em-dashes (JL 260724): use a colon, a semicolon, a comma, parentheses, or simply start a new sentence.

**Voice**: Declarative, not tentative.
State what the page rules rather than what someone might consider.
Do not hedge with "should probably" or "we might".
A ruling that is JL's carries his name and the date, written as `(JL 260801)`, so that a later reader can tell a decision from a description.

**Opening is one two-part pitch**: Write one question lead, then one plain rationale paragraph.
Name what this page gives the reader, not how it is implemented.
Write for a cold reader who arrived from one link with no prior context.
Define the question's own words before anything else (JL 260801): when the lead question uses this board's vocabulary, the rationale opens by making each of those terms concrete, one line each, with a real example rather than a restatement.
A reader who cannot parse the question cannot be argued with, so this sits above the bearing rule rather than beside it.
Place the page on the board before arguing for it (JL 260801): name the neighbouring page the reader most likely came from and say what it handles, so a newcomer can tell which rung they are standing on.
Two or three sentences of that bearing are enough, and they belong at the top of the rationale, because a reader who cannot locate the page cannot judge the case for it.
Carry ONE central idea; the framework belongs in Diagram and Content.
There is no required sentence count and no required rhetorical order.
Questions such as why this matters, what makes it difficult, what remains broken, and what it affects are review probes, not sentence slots.
Stop when the reader understands the page-specific stake and knows why the rest is worth reading.
Never fill the house skeleton: "This page defines X", "The hard part is", "Without that", "It succeeds when" (JL 260801).
Those four slots turned the rule into a mad-lib: 37 of this board's 53 pages ended on "succeeds when" and 22 opened on "This page", so the pages differed only by one noun.
The check is a paste test: if the rationale would still read correctly on a neighbouring page after swapping its subject noun, it has said nothing and must be rewritten in this page's own words.

**The opening paragraph**: Everything before the FIRST BLANK LINE in `## Opening` is what a reader sees without clicking; everything after it drops into the `More details` drawer (JL 260801).
Keep it to 4 or 5 sentences, about five lines on screen, under roughly 450 characters.
Write it in plain English for a reader whose English is weak: a shorter common word always beats a precise rare one.
Its shape is the question, then what the question's own words mean with a real example each, then why that is hard, then what this page decides.
Never open with a list that will grow: name examples and say the set grows, so a fourth member never forces an edit here.

**Every figure carries a caption**: One line directly ABOVE the fence, written as `**Name**: what this diagram shows.` (JL 260801).
A section may hold several figures, and an unlabelled one makes the reader decode it before knowing what it is.

**A figure row is a label and its value**: Never a clause (JL 260801).
If a row could end in a period it is prose, and it belongs in the paragraph under the figure rather than inside the fence.

**Every Content division opens with a face figure**: The first fenced block after a `### ` heading is that division's face diagram, and it is drawn with `/diagram-ascii`.
Make it emoji-dense, with boxes and arrows showing the division's SHAPE at a glance.
It is not the contract table, which follows it.
Keep it under about 80 characters wide so it survives being pasted into a chat or a terminal.
Remember that a figure is a picture rather than a paragraph in a box.

**Inside a Content division**: After the face figure comes a one or two line intro saying what the division establishes, emoji welcome, never a paragraph.
Then write it as `####` paragraph headings, each with a one-line job in parentheses, or as **Item**: prose paragraphs with one bold lead-in per beat.
Pick one shape and stay with it rather than alternating.

**Below the lead**: Everything after the FIRST BLANK LINE renders in the `More details` fold, and it is written as a list of labelled parts, never one block of prose.
The on-stage paragraph above that blank line is written as consecutive one-sentence source lines with no blank line between them, because the renderer joins them into one block.
Use the review probes above to find omissions, never as a mold that every page must fill.
Do not use bold Item beats here; those belong in Content.

**Content is numbered all the way down**: A division is `### 3 · Content`, a group inside it is `**3.2 · Group title**`, and a paragraph is `#### 3.2.1 · Its heading` (JL 260801).
An ungrouped division numbers its paragraphs `#### 3.1 ·` straight through, so the DEPTH of the number says whether a group exists, which is the same rule the board already uses for `§6` against `§6.1`.
Every number is followed by ` · ` and the heading's own words.
The index is what makes a long division navigable and citable: `1.2.3` is a thing a person can point at in chat, and a heading alone is not.

**Content**: Numbered divisions, `### 0 ·`, `### 1 ·`, and so on, one subject per division.
A division may hold `####` subsections when it genuinely carries more than one idea, and it stays a single block when it does not.
Content holds only what this page establishes.
Scope belongs in Opening, intended outcomes belong in Aims, and current facts belong in States.

**Aims**: Durable target states linked to Content divisions by stable ids.
Each Aim has a testable `Done when`; an optional `Plan` records the current route without becoming a third fixed section.
Aims carry no checkboxes and no progress claims.

**States**: The honest present, and nothing aspirational.
Mirror every Aim id exactly once with ⬜, 🔨, 🧠, ✅, or ❄️.
Human choices live in `Decision Now`; reasons for status transitions run newest first in Log.

**Files**: Only the few files someone opens to continue this work, each with one line saying what role it plays.
Mark generated files explicitly so that nobody edits the wrong layer.
It is an action map rather than an exhaustive change list.

**Section names verbatim**: `build.py` keys on the whole string after `## `, so an annotated or renamed heading silently renders nothing at all.
Required and optional markers belong in the first body line, never in the heading itself.

**Stale sentences are defects, not history**: When a ruling changes, every sentence that now contradicts it is corrected in the same edit.
`## Log` is where the earlier state is remembered, and it runs newest first.

**3.1 · 📦 What Content holds, and what it does not**

#### 3.1.1 · Content is what the page makes, not the story of making it
(write what is true now, never how you got there)
This is the rule the rest of `### 3` rests on, and it is the one broken most often.
Write the conclusion, in the present tense, as a thing that is now true and can be acted on.
How the decision was reached goes in `## Log`.
The argument about it goes in `## Discussion`.
What went wrong on the way goes in `## Lesson`.
A part that reads like a story of attempts has become a diary, and a diary cannot be checked, only re-read.

#### 3.1.2 · A sentence that answers another section's question is a defect
(and the page already says where it goes)
Scope goes to Opening. Required Inputs and Venue go to Stage Contract. Rules about writing go to `## Writing Style`.
A fact about right now goes to States. A result you are trying to reach goes to Aims.
The test is mechanical, not a matter of taste: ask which reader question the sentence answers, then put it where that question is answered.

#### 3.1.3 · Read the writing rules before you write Content
(the page has already said how it must be written)
`## Writing Style` holds the rules for this page, and every page has one.
A stage page also has a Stage Contract, which brings Required Inputs, the venue, and what the stage before it expects.
Anything those imply about writing is copied into this page's own `## Writing Style`, so there is never a second place to look.
Read them first. Content written against the wrong rules has to be rewritten rather than edited, and you find that out after the work is done.

**3.2 · 🗂 What one part of Content must be**

#### 3.2.1 · One part answers one question
(a title that needs the word "and" is two parts)
Each SECTION of the page answers one reader question, and inside Content the same rule applies one level down.
Give the part a title that states its question or its claim, then let the body answer only that.
When you start needing "also" and "separately", it is two parts.
Splitting costs nothing, because parts are cheap.

#### 3.2.2 · One part is one piece of the thing the page makes
(so what counts as a piece depends on what the page makes)
A part may hold several paragraphs.
On a page whose product is a real artifact, one part is one piece of it.
A section of a paper. A panel of a dashboard. A message in a thread.
So a part is not an arbitrary chunk of text. It is the shape the product already has.
Let the artifact decide where a part starts and stops, instead of splitting by how much you have written.

#### 3.2.3 · A part must be able to reach a final form
(if it can never be finished, it is an Aim wearing a Content part's clothes)
Content holds settled material, so every part should be the kind of thing that can be finished.
A result you are trying to reach goes in `## Aims`.
Progress, blockers, open decisions, and current facts go in `## States`.
A part you will be editing forever is usually a status report that wandered in.

#### 3.2.4 · A part must make sense to someone who jumped straight to it
(readers arrive from the page list, not from the top)
Parts are numbered so they can be pointed at, and the page list makes any of them the first thing a reader sees.
So a part may not lean on the one above it for its meaning.
Name the thing again instead of writing "as described above".
Read in order they should still feel like an argument, but no single one may fall apart when read cold.

**3.3 · ✍️ How a part is written**

#### 3.3.1 · Every part opens with a caption, a figure, and a short intro
(all three, in that order, before any prose)
Draw the figure with `/diagram-ascii`, and make it emoji-dense: one on every box, every row label, every status mark.
It shows the SHAPE of what this part settles, in about 80 characters or less, so it survives being pasted into a chat or a terminal.
Above it goes one caption line, `**Name**: what this diagram shows.`, because a reader meeting an unlabelled figure starts working it out before any explanation arrives.
`### 2` holds the full rules for drawing one, including the arrow vocabulary and the label-and-value row rule.
Then one or two lines saying what this part settles, so a reader who jumped straight here knows what they are inside before any detail arrives.
Keep it a signpost, not a summary. If it needs a third line, it is competing with the part itself.
Emoji are welcome, for the same reason the figures carry them: they make a short line quick to scan.

#### 3.3.2 · Add a level only when there is more than one idea
(and the number carries the depth, so a reader can see it)
A whole-line `**bold**` is a group title, written `**3.2 · Title**`, and it must lead a real group rather than float above one paragraph.
A `####` is a paragraph heading, written `#### 3.2.1 · Heading`, with its own one-line job underneath in parentheses.
A part holding exactly one `####` has a heading it does not need: either it has more to say, or the heading goes.
Below that, one sentence per line, and each line can carry its own note.

#### 3.3.3 · Evidence attaches to the sentence that makes the claim
(a source, a number, or a question gets answered without breaking the sentence)
Put a `>` line directly under a sentence and it belongs to THAT sentence, not to the paragraph and not to the page.
The sentence stays clean, and one click opens everything attached to it.
Three kinds can hang there, and the badge at the end of the sentence says which one:

⚑ **A typed lane** names what it attaches. There are eight:

📚 `> Citation:` a source · 🔢 `> Value:` a number and where it came from · 🖼 `> Display:` a figure or table

⚠️ `> Check:` something still to verify · 🔎 `> Q-consumer:` a question this sentence needs answered

🔗 `> Link:` · 📄 `> Source:` · 📝 `> Note:` for anything the five above do not cover

💬 **A comment** is a person talking, written `> Comment JL …`, and it means someone is waiting on an answer.

✎ **A change record** is an edit, written `> ✎ ~old~ *new* · WHO · YYMMDD HHMM`, and it shows which words moved.

The badge reads `⚑ 2` for two typed lanes, `💬 2` when two people are waiting, and `✎ 2` for two edits.
`> Comment JL …` is the only form to write (JL 260802).
It was `> JL: …`, which still renders so nothing already written breaks, and `check.py` warns on it.
Beside `> Citation:` and `> Value:`, a bare pair of initials told a newcomer nothing about what the row was.
`## Discussion` is NOT affected and keeps `> JL:` with `>> CC0726:` replies: that is a THREAD, a different grammar in a different section, and 156 rows across the boards use it.
A person waiting outranks a record, so a sentence carrying both shows `💬`.
This is another reason Content holds the product and not the story: a note attached to a sentence about what someone tried has nothing stable to point at.
The three lanes `Citation`, `Value`, and `Display` map one to one onto the paper placeholders `\cite{TOADD}`, `{VAL:? …}`, and a display id, which is how this board reaches `/haipipe-paper`.

#### 3.3.4 · Which page owns which part of the sentence
(this part says evidence attaches HERE; the QB5 family says how it all works)
`QB5` is the front door: what the sentence is, and why it is the board's smallest unit.
`QB5a` owns the evidence card itself, the lane grammar, the ⚑ badge, and how the drawer renders.
`QB5b` owns comments, `QB5c` owns editing a sentence from the page, and `QB5d` owns chatting about one location.
`QB5e` owns the lifecycle: when a record appears, when it is answered, and when it stops being shown.
This part owns only the rule that evidence attaches in Content, and why.

**3.4 · 🧬 Who fills Content**

#### 3.4.1 · A page kind owns Content, and nothing else
(a "kind" is a family of pages that hold the same sort of material)
Start with the word, because the rest of this part leans on it.
A page KIND is a family of pages that hold the same sort of material: decision pages are one kind, lifecycle-stage pages are another, skill-definition pages are a third, and `3.4.2` describes all three.
Every kind uses the same seven sections in the same order.
What changes between one kind and the next is Content, and only Content.

A kind declares three things.
What its Content holds, and who writes it when the page is created.
Which records it adds through Aims, States, or Stage Contract.
And how the page closes.
It defines Content. It never redefines, adds, removes, or reorders the other sections.
It may not change the folds, change the sentence grammar, or touch the render code.
That line is guarded one layer down: `src/dialect_paper.py` opens with "THIS MODULE IS DELETABLE", and `build.py` loads that route only when a board asks for `dialect: paper`.

#### 3.4.2 · The kinds that exist today
(three so far, and the list is open: a fourth gets written down here)
A decision page's Content is optional and free-shaped, and nobody composes it. The author writes it.
A lifecycle-stage page's Content is required, and its consumer writes it when the page is created, for example `haipipe-paper-stage/create-page.py`.
Board `stage.py` owns the generic page and keeps the inherited contract in sync.
A skill-definition page is generated from a definition file inside a managed block, which is why it needed no renderer work at all.
The three names are fixed.
The Opening, this part, and `## Law` all use the same ones, because a kind that answers to two names cannot be looked up.

### 4 · Aims
**Aims map onto Content**: durable targets, grouped by the division they serve.

```
🎯 Aims · what should become TRUE, and for which part of Content?

  📚 ### 3 · Content        ━━▶  🎯 ### A3 · 📚 Content   ━━▶  📍 ### A3 · 📚 Content
     the part itself                A3.1 · target state          ✅ A3.1 · what is true now
                                    A3.2 · target state          🔨 A3.2 · what is true now

  🔢 same NUMBER · 🏷 same NAME · 🎨 same EMOJI, in all three
  🏁 ### P · page-level        for a target that belongs to no single part

  🎯 an Aim ── stays true when the route changes
      ├── ✅ Done when: a condition someone else can check
      └── 🗺 Plan: the current route, optional, never its own section

  🚦 the stamp on each row comes FROM States, never from the Aim's own markdown
     ⬜ not started   🔨 working now   🧠 waiting on a person
     ✅ met, evidence named            ❄️ on ice, held on purpose

  ❌ a task: "run the test"      ✅ an Aim: "the contract survives a cold read"
  ❌ a checkbox in Aims          ✅ the status emoji lives only in States
```
🎯 Establishes the page's durable target model and ties intent back to Content.

```
🎯 Aims · answers: what should become true, and for which Content division?
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
👁 conveys   intended target states, independent of the current route
📦 holds     A<n>.<n> records grouped under the matching Content division ·
             P<n> only for a genuine page-level target · Done when · optional Plan
✍️ source    `- A3.1 · target` followed by a testable `Done when` line
⚖️ rules     no checkboxes · no present-tense progress · one division may have
             zero, one, or many Aims · intent changes are recorded in Log
⏭ omit      never · without Aims the page cannot say what it is trying to achieve
```

**4.1 · 🎯 What an Aim is, and what it is not**

#### 4.1.1 · An Aim says what should become true; its State says what is true now
(one sentence each, and neither may do the other's job)
This is the boundary the whole section rests on, so test any line against it before writing it anywhere.
An Aim survives a change of route, of owner, and of what you are doing next week: if a line stops being true merely because today's plan changed, it was a Plan and not an Aim.
A State reports what is the case right now, with evidence, and it changes often.
The pair is what makes the page checkable: intent that never moves, beside fact that moves constantly, sharing one id so a reader can hold them side by side.
Put progress, a blocker, a person you are waiting on, or a piece of evidence in States. An Aim that reports any of those has started doing States' job, and the reader now has two places to look for one answer.

The markdown of an Aim carries NO status, and the page shows one anyway: each row is stamped at render with what States says about it (JL 260802).
That keeps one writer and one reader: the status is written once, in States, and shown wherever it is useful.
Five statuses exist, `### 5` owns them, and this is what each means when you see it on an Aim row:

⬜ not started · 🔨 being worked on now · 🧠 waiting on a person or on something outside this page

✅ met, with the evidence named · ❄️ on ice: deliberately held, and thawable

🧠 is the one worth reading carefully: waiting is not failing, and it usually pairs with a row under `States › Decision Now` naming the choice somebody has to make.

#### 4.1.2 · What is not an Aim
(four things that look like one, and where each belongs)
A TASK is not an Aim: "run the checker" is something you do, and "the checker reports zero errors on this board" is something that becomes true.
A THING ALREADY TRUE is not an Aim: if nothing is left to establish, it is settled material for Content, and its State row is the only place it appears.
A QUESTION is not an Aim: a question waiting on a person is a `Decision Now` row in States, and the Aim beside it states the OUTCOME the ruling has to establish, never the question.
A WORK HISTORY is not an Aim either. Remove or revise an Aim only when the intended outcome itself changes, and record that change in Log; completed work is visible through States, so an Aim that accumulates what was done stops being readable as intent months later.

**4.2 · 🔢 How an Aim is addressed**

#### 4.2.1 · A group is `A<n>`, and it points at Content part `<n>`
(the group holds A-records, so the group carries an A)
An Aims group is written `### A3 · 📚 Content`, and the matching States group is written exactly the same way.
The number is the Content part it answers, the name is that part's name copied, and the emoji is that part's emoji, so the three sections line up by number, by name, and by eye.
The correspondence is by ID, not by count: `A3` may hold several Aims or none, and a part with nothing left to establish simply has no group.
That is deliberate. Forcing one Aim per part would fill the section with records nobody needs, and an empty record is indistinguishable from a met one.
Use `P` when the target genuinely spans the page and belongs to no single part.
It was `C3` until 260802, which made a reader translate one letter into another to see that `A3.1` belonged under it; `C<n>` still resolves, because older boards are full of it.

#### 4.2.2 · An Aim may point outside the page
(the work can be in a template, the engine, or another skill; the Aim still belongs to the part that states the rule)
A page rules things that live elsewhere: `A8.2` is about `ref/page-template.md`, and `A9.1` is about a review path in two other skills.
Those are proper Aims and they need no id of their own.
An Aim belongs to the part that STATES the rule, wherever the work happens, because that is the part a reader is standing in when they ask whether it holds.
An Aim whose work is entirely elsewhere still needs a `Done when` that can be checked FROM HERE, which usually means naming the file or the command that proves it.

**4.3 · ✅ What makes an Aim checkable**

#### 4.3.1 · Where an Aim comes from
(the gap between what Content now says and what is actually true)
Do not invent Aims. Read Content, then ask of each part: is what this says already true everywhere it should be?
Where the answer is no, that gap is the Aim, and the part it sits in gives it its number.
This is why Aims go stale quietly. A night of ruling can add eight rules to Content and leave Aims untouched, and nothing reports it, because a missing Aim looks exactly like a finished one.
So write the Aim in the same edit as the rule. If the rule is worth stating, whether anything obeys it is worth recording.

#### 4.3.2 · `Done when` is the whole test, and `Plan` is optional
(if nobody else can check it, it is not written yet)
`Done when` states a condition another person can verify without asking you what you meant.
Name the file, the count, the command, or the run: `check.py reports zero division-no-figure findings on this board` can be checked; `figures are consistent` cannot.
It is what separates an Aim from a wish, and it is what lets an Aim point outside the page at all, because the check is the only thing that travels back.
`Plan` names the current route, and only when writing it down helps someone continue. It stays subordinate to its Aim and never becomes a third top-level section, because a route changes while the target does not.
When the route changes, rewrite Plan and leave the Aim alone. If you find yourself rewriting the Aim instead, it was a Plan.

#### 4.3.3 · An Aim that needs a person keeps its id
(authority moves; the target does not)
Mark it 🧠 in States, and pair it with a row under `States › Decision Now` carrying the options and a recommendation.
The Aim itself does not change: it goes on stating the outcome the ruling has to establish, so that whichever option is chosen, the same line says whether the page got there.
Only the person closes the decision, and the State row then moves from 🧠 to whatever became true.

### 5 · States
**One row per Aim**: the five statuses, and where a human decision goes.

```
📍 States · what is factually true for every Aim right now?

  🎯 A1.1 ──▶ ⬜ not started
  🎯 A1.2 ──▶ 🔨 active
  🎯 A2.1 ──▶ 🧠 waiting on human / external dependency
  🎯 A3.1 ──▶ ✅ met, with evidence in Content or Files
  🎯 P1   ──▶ ❄️ explicitly held

  one Aim id ──▶ exactly one current row
  group names mirror Aims and Content

  🗣 Decision Now   options + recommendation for a 🧠 Aim
  📜 Log            why a State changed; history does not clutter the snapshot
```
📍 Establishes the factual snapshot, the status vocabulary, and the human-decision edge.

```
📍 States · answers: what is true now for each Aim, and what is waiting?
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
👁 conveys   the honest present, one status per Aim
📦 holds     mirrored groups and Aim ids · current status rows · Decision Now
✍️ source    `- 🔨 A3.1 · factual status`, with evidence named when met
⚖️ rules     no aspirations · no duplicate current rows · transitions go to Log ·
             States groups and Aim ids must resolve back to Aims
⏭ omit      never · a page that cannot report its Aim States is unreadable
```

**5.1 · 🚦 What a State row says**

#### 5.1.1 · The five statuses, and what each one admits
(one per Aim, and the row is only as useful as the honesty in it)
`⬜` not started, `🔨` being worked on now, `🧠` waiting on a person or on something outside this page, `✅` met with the evidence named, and `❄️` on ice: held on purpose and thawable.
Each says its meaning by SHAPE (JL 260802). The old set carried two of the five in hue alone: `🟡` and `🟠` are one circle in two colours, which is no difference in greyscale or to a colour-blind reader.
`🧠` is the glyph the `owner:` line already uses for the person who decides, so waiting-on-a-person reuses vocabulary the reader has met.
`🟡` `🟠` `⏸️` still parse, because rows on other boards use them.
`✅` is the one people lie with. It means the evidence exists and is named in the row, not that the work felt finished.

#### 5.1.2 · A State reports, it does not re-argue
(the Aim already said what should be true; this says what is)
Every Aim appears here exactly once, under the same group, with the same id.
Say what is the case and point at the proof: a count, a file, a run, a command someone else can repeat.
Do not restate the target and do not rehearse Content's argument. A reader comparing the two lists wants the difference between them, and a State that paraphrases its Aim hides exactly that.
When a State changes, edit the one row and add a dated line to Log. Two rows for one Aim means the page is now claiming two things at once.

**5.2 · 🗣 Decision Now**

#### 5.2.1 · Decision Now comes FIRST, above the status rows
(it is the only part of this section that asks the reader to do something)
Everything else in States is a report; this is a request.
A reader who opens States is usually asking "what needs me?", and putting that at the bottom under nine groups of status rows answers it last.
So `### Decision Now` is the first subsection, and the per-Aim groups follow it (JL 260802).

#### 5.2.2 · Most proposed decisions do not belong here
(the rows that survive are the ones nobody cared enough to answer)
Read this page's own record before adding a row. On 260801 and 260802 about 46 decisions were made and written into Log, and NOT ONE of them went through Decision Now: they were settled in conversation within minutes of being raised.
Meanwhile the two rows sitting in Decision Now had been unanswered since 260801.
That is the pattern, and it is worth stating plainly: a decision that matters gets made almost immediately, so Decision Now accumulates the residue rather than the substance.
The cause is that decisions are proposed by the agent, and an agent can generate a plausible fork about anything. The standing rule is the opposite one: decide it, do it, and record it in Log.
So the test is not "is this a choice?" but "does anything stop until it is answered?".

#### 5.2.3 · A decision lives on the page, never in a chat session
(the session is gone tomorrow; the page is what a person opens)
Never ask for a ruling only in chat (JL 260802: "don't put the decision in the claude code session").
Write the row FIRST, in the owning page's `States › Decision Now`, and then say in chat which page and which row. The chat line is a pointer, not the decision.
Do not hunt for the page by hand: `haipipe-board-routing` takes the decision, reads `board.md`'s `## Pages` as the only registry, and lands it on the owning page and section.
It writes the row, and it closes one only after the person has answered it; see `### 5.2.6`.
Three things fail when it lives in a session instead. Nobody else can see it, so a decision that needs a second person cannot reach them. It has no `Blocks:` and no `Default:`, so an unanswered question just evaporates rather than resolving itself. And it leaves no record of the options that were weighed, which is what the next person to reopen the question actually needs.
The test is simple: if it is a question only YOU can answer, it is a row. If it is a question I should have answered myself, `### 5.2.2` says decide it and put it in Log.

#### 5.2.4 · Every row names what it blocks, and carries a default
(a decision with no default is a decision that will sit)
🛑 `Blocks:` says what cannot proceed. If the honest answer is nothing, write nothing, and then the row must carry a default.
That is what keeps this list short: a non-blocking decision resolves itself, the ruling goes to Log as CC's, and the person can overturn it later like any other ruling.
A blocking decision has no default, because guessing would produce work that has to be thrown away, which is the only thing worth stopping for.

#### 5.2.5 · The row's shape, and why it looks like this
(five marked fields and a list of options; the question is the row's title)
The question is the row's own heading, so there is no separate `Ask:` line to read past.
Each field carries an emoji, because nine identical `Label:` rows give the eye nothing to sort the question from the options from the consequence.
The recommendation is a ⭐ on the option itself rather than a line at the bottom repeating a letter, which removes a row and lets a reader find the answer without reading to the end.
The letters stay, because a person says "go with B" and needs something to point at.

🗣 the question, as the row's title

📍 `Part` which Content part it belongs to, so it can be read beside the rule it changes

🔔 `Why now` what raised it, in a sentence

⭐ `A ·` `B ·` … one option per line, each naming its CONSEQUENCE rather than its label, with the star on the one CC recommends and the reason in that line

🛑 `Blocks` what stops until it is answered, or `nothing`

🤖 `If nobody answers` the option that takes effect, required whenever nothing is blocked

#### 5.2.6 · Give it as many options as it has, never three by habit
(two is a real decision; three padded to look considered is not)
A yes-or-no question has two options and should show two.
Some have four, and a rename with three live candidates has three.
Never invent a third to look thorough: a filler option is worse than none, because the reader has to read it before discovering it was filler, and it makes the two real ones look less considered.
If you cannot state a second option that someone might actually choose, it is not a decision. Decide it and write it in Log.

#### 5.2.7 · An answered decision leaves States entirely
(States holds a request and a report, and an answered decision is neither)
When the person chooses, the row does not move down the page. It splits, and both halves land below the read.

⚖️ the RULING goes to `## Law`, carrying the date, the person, their own words where they gave them, AND the options that were not chosen with the reason. That last part is what the next person to reopen the question actually needs, and a Log line does not preserve it.

📜 the CHANGE goes to `## Log`, one dated line saying what moved.

📍 the matching Aim's State row updates in the same edit, since the whole reason the decision existed was that a State could not be honest without it.

There was a subsection holding answered decisions here until 260802. It was history sitting inside a snapshot, which `5.3.1` forbids two paragraphs below it, and three of its four entries were already duplicated in Log.

#### 5.2.6 · A machine closes a row the person has already answered
(an answered row left open is a page lying about what is pending)
JL 260802: "I think you should close it automatically, please go ahead and do it."
Until then a machine wrote rows and never ticked one, so a row answered in chat and acted on within the hour still rendered as pending, and the page reported work as waiting that had already shipped. That is the drift the board exists to prevent, arriving through the one section meant to belong to the person.
So the machine closes the row and records the answer in the same write: which option, who ruled, when, and the words they used.
The line it may not cross is unchanged, and it is what keeps the ruling the person's. It may not close a row nobody answered, and it may not flip a page-level human gate.
Answered means the person said it, in chat, in a comment lane, or by ticking. A machine's own recommendation is never an answer, however confident the ⭐ was.

**5.3 · 📜 What does not belong in States**

#### 5.3.1 · History belongs in Log
(States is a snapshot, not a stack of snapshots)
A superseded State stays findable in Log or Law; it does not sit beside the current one as though both were true.
The section should be readable top to bottom as a description of right now, and every line that survives from an earlier now makes that false.

### 6 · Files
**The action map**: the three file groups, chosen by what you DO to a file.

```
📎 Files  ── the action map, NOT a change list

  ⚙️ Engines        what RUNS the subject      🔧 build.py · serve.py
  📥 Input files    what the work READS        📄 the .md sources
  📤 Output files   what a build WRITES        🌐 board/*.html   ⚠️ never hand-edit

🧭 which group? ── by what YOU DO to it, not by what it IS
   the same .md is 📥 input on one page and 📤 output on another
```
📎 Establishes the action map: the standard trio, and which group a file belongs to.

```
📎 Files · answers: which few files do I open to continue this work?
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
👁 conveys   the action map: entry points, never an exhaustive change list
📦 holds     the standard trio: Engines, what runs the subject · Input files,
             what the work reads · Output files, what a build writes
✍️ source    one backticked path + its role in one sentence, per row ·
             a generated file says so in its row: never hand-edit
⚖️ rules     place a file by what YOU do to it, not by what it is · rename a
             group only when the trio truly misfits · omit an empty group ·
             three rows or fewer stay flat
⏭ omit      allowed but strongly advised against: a page with no Files
             leaves the next person searching
```

**6.1 · 📎 What this section is for**

#### 6.1.1 · It answers one question: if I change a rule here, what do I touch?
(not "files about this topic", which is a different and much longer list)
A page states rules. Those rules are implemented somewhere, and Files is the map from one to the other.
Tonight's work is the shape of it: every rule written on this page landed in the same five kinds of place, which are the page itself, `ref/page-template.md` so new pages inherit it, `haipipe-board-page` so a reviewer can apply it, the renderer that makes it visible, and `check.py` that catches a page breaking it.
A reader who has just read a rule and disagrees with it needs exactly that list.
So the test for a row is not "is this file related?" but "would changing what this page says mean editing this file?".

#### 6.1.2 · The action map, not a change list
(the few files someone opens to continue, each with one line saying its role)
Completeness is not the goal.
An exhaustive list is worse than a short one, because it hides the entry points among files nobody needs.
One line per row saying what that file does, and nothing else. A path with no role is a path the reader has to open to understand.

**6.2 · 🗂 How the rows are grouped**

#### 6.2.1 · Group by what you DO to the file, not by what it is
(the same file is an input on one page and an output on another)
⚙️ Engines run the subject, 📥 Inputs are what the work reads, and 📤 Outputs are what a build writes.
`board.md` is an input to the renderer and an output of the index verb, so its own nature never settles the question. The page's relationship to it does.
An empty group is omitted, and three rows or fewer stay flat with no group at all.

#### 6.2.2 · The group names are a MENU of actions, and a page takes what applies
(five names cover almost everything; a page uses the ones it has and omits the rest)
⚙️ `Engines` what RUNS the subject · 📋 `Contracts` what CARRIES a rule to other pages · 🧪 `Checks` what CATCHES a page breaking one · 📥 `Input files` what the work READS · 📤 `Output files` what a BUILD writes.
Take the ones that apply. This page has no meaningful Inputs group, so it does not have one, and a page that only reads and writes uses two.
`Contracts` and `Checks` exist because a spec page needs them and the older trio had nowhere to put them: `ref/page-template.md` is not something a build writes, and `cli/check.py` runs but what this page DOES to it is write rules into it.
A page may still add a name, and the test is that it states an ACTION. `The Board mark sources` and `Graduation target and neighbouring faces` are subjects, and a subject-named group rots the moment its subject leaves the page, which is exactly what happened here on 260801.
`### 0.5`'s contextual-naming rule holds: it asks for the page's own language, and an action in the page's own words is still an action.

#### 6.2.3 · Mark what is generated
(the failure this prevents is someone editing the layer that gets overwritten)
A generated file says so in its own row, in the same breath as its path.

**6.3 · ⏳ Why this section rots**

#### 6.3.1 · A missing row is the failure no check can find
(a dead path is mechanical; an absent one is a judgment)
`check.py` resolves every path written here, so a row that rots is caught.
Nothing catches a file that SHOULD be listed and is not, because "should this be here" is the same question `### 6.1.1` asks, and only a person reading the rules can answer it.
The wrong instrument is a diff. Tonight's work touched 53 files under `skills/board/`, and 52 of them do not belong here: most are another session's, and the rest are files nobody changing a rule on this page would ever open.
Exactly one belonged, `agents/haipipe-board-reviewer-agent.md`, because `### 9.3.3` makes it the thing that applies these rules.
So audit this section by re-reading `### 6.1.1`'s question against each row, not by listing what changed.

#### 6.3.2 · Paths move, and nothing on the page notices
(a stale Files section is worse than none, because it sends the reader somewhere that no longer exists)
Prose can be wrong and still read as prose. A path is either right or it is a dead end, and the reader only finds out after following it.
This page proves the rate: of fourteen paths listed here, FOUR were dead by 260802. `build.py` and `stage.py` had moved into `cli/`, `assets/board.css` had been split into `assets/css/*.css`, and `test_stage_style.py` was gone.
A group can rot too. `The Board mark sources` survived here after 260801 deleted the Content part it served, so it pointed at files no part of this page discusses.
`check.py` now resolves every path in this section, which is the only reason to trust the rows: a dead path is a mechanical fact and does not need a person to notice it.

**7.1 · 🗃 What lives down here**

#### 7.1.1 · Memory that must not tax the first read
(always below the reading path, always starting shut)
Discussion holds the deliberation, Law the binding rules, Lesson what failure taught, Glossary the words, and Log the history.
They are the same five on every page kind, and an empty one never renders.
Adding a sixth is a template decision made here, never something one page invents for itself.

#### 7.1.2 · Discussion is a nested thread, newest first
(the shape Reddit and Discord use, because that is what a thread of replies is)
Each entry shows an avatar with the speaker's initials, the name, and what they said, all on ONE row, so a reader learns what an exchange is about without reading down a column of names.
A reply is nested INSIDE the entry it answers, so the page list on the left runs the full height of everything below it and a reader can see where one exchange ends.
`⊖` collapses a subtree and `⊕` reopens it, done with `<details>` so it still works with every script stripped. A collapsed entry keeps its first line, which is why the name and the line share a row.
The newest thread renders FIRST, for the same reason Log runs newest first: the source stays chronological because that is how a person appends to a file, and the reader wants the current state. Only the top level reverses, since reversing inside a thread would put an answer before its question.
The form for adding a thought sits ABOVE the thread, so writing a reply never means scrolling past the whole history.
Depth comes from the run of `>` already in the source: `>` opens a thread, `>>` replies to it, and no page had to be rewritten to gain any of this.
The author is optional and, when present, is followed by a colon. Without that rule the initials pattern matched the first letter of any capitalised word, and `> Open: how does a record attach…` rendered as a speaker called `O` saying `pen: how does…`.

#### 7.1.3 · The other four folds, and what each one refuses
(a record filed wrong is a record lost)
Law holds what BINDS. Write it so a later reader can tell a ruling from a description, with the person and the date on it, and with the options that were rejected.
Lesson holds what failure taught, and its test is that it generalizes. The fix goes in Log; the reason the fix was needed goes here.
Glossary holds the words this page uses in a sense a newcomer would not guess.
Log holds what changed and why, one dated line per change.

#### 7.1.4 · Log is the only one with an order
(newest first, because a reader arriving cold wants the current state, not the origin story)
Every other fold is a set, so its entries can be arranged however reads best.
A Log line carries the date, what changed, and the reason, and it names the person when the change was a ruling rather than an implementation.
It is also the only place the page may contradict itself. A Log line records what was true when it was written and is never rewritten, and that is exactly what makes the stale-sentence rule enforceable everywhere else.

**7.2 · ✍️ How to write one**

#### 7.2.1 · Every entry is a list row with an emoji, and a Glossary term is bold
(a fold is scanned before it is read, and a wall of identical lines cannot be scanned)
Write each Law, Lesson and Glossary entry as a `- ` row that opens with an emoji, which the render lifts out as the row's icon (JL 260802).
The emoji says the SUBJECT, so a reader hunting the figure rules finds 🖼 without reading forty headings: 🏷 title · 📎 Files · 🎯 Aims · 📍 States · 🧭 Opening · 📚 Content · 🗃 folds · 💬 comments · ⚖️ for a rule with no narrower home.
A Glossary term is bold and its definition follows a colon, because the term is what a reader arrived looking for and the definition is what they read once they have found it.
The point is that these three are opened by someone who already has a question. They are not read top to bottom, so the first thing on every row has to be the thing that says whether this row is the one.

#### 7.2.2 · Write these for a reader who is already stuck
(they opened this because the page above did not answer their question)
Nobody opens Law for pleasure, so an entry has to survive being read out of order, with no memory of the page above it.
Name the thing again instead of writing "the rule described above".
State the ruling first and the reason after, because the reader is checking one specific thing and will stop as soon as they have it.
That is the same standing-alone discipline `### 3` asks of a Content part, applied to the material that is hardest to write for.

#### 7.2.3 · A note about one sentence stays with that sentence
(it hides under its own sentence, never down here at the bottom)
Prose inside a fold is not read-only either: a sentence in Discussion, Law, Lesson or Glossary takes a comment exactly like main text, because the anchor rule lands on any plain source line whatever section it is in.
What still refuses is what cannot anchor: rendered comment rows, a sentence's own attached notes, and Log rows, which are records rather than sentences.

### 8 · The source file
**Template to page**: the one file you copy, and the rules build.py silently enforces.

```
📄 ref/page-template.md       the deliverable: adding a page = copying this
        |
        | 📋 copy + rename
        v
📝 Q<id>-<slug>.md            🔑 md is the ONLY source of truth
   🏷 title · state · owner    ── required identity, becomes the index row
      └ 🎯 sentence case · says what the page is FOR, not just its topic
   ✅🟡🔴⏸️ first emoji         ── the machine status, detail may follow
        |
        | ⚙️ build.py    ── keys on the WHOLE string after "## "
        v
🌐 board/<group>/<page>.html  ⚠️ generated · never hand-edit

🚫 "## Opening (required)"  ── silently yields NOTHING; nothing reports it
🚫 "Page Template design"   ── two cases, and it names a topic rather than a purpose
```

**8.1 · 🏷 The head of the file**

#### 8.1.1 · The metadata head is identity
(title, state and owner become the index row and the page header)
`method` is optional.
The first emoji on the `state:` line is the machine status and must be ✅, 🟡, 🔴 or ⏸️. Readable detail may follow it, so `✅ PINNED · MISQ 2026` is the same machine state as `✅ SETTLED`.
That is the PAGE state vocabulary, and it is not the Aim vocabulary in `### 5`. The two share glyphs, mean different things, and are checked apart.

#### 8.1.2 · The title says what the page is FOR
(a topic name tells a reader where they are; a purpose tells them what to do with it)
It is the one line every reader meets before deciding whether to open the page, and on the Index it is the only line they get.
So write the purpose into it. A colon after the opening phrase is usually where that lands, as in `The code's shape: one Law, three files`.
A title naming only its subject makes the reader open the page to learn what the page was for, which is the work the title existed to save them.

#### 8.1.3 · The title is a phrase in sentence case
(capitalize the first word and proper nouns, and nothing else)
Title Case is not an alternative convention here, because an Index carrying both reads as two boards merged by accident.
A defined term keeps its capitals, so `one Law, three files` and `Mounting a SPACE` are already correct.
This page's own title broke the rule the day it was reworded: the edit replaced half of `Single Question Webpage Layout` and left `Page Template` standing beside a freshly typed lower-case `design`, and no rule existed for anything to check the seam against.

**8.2 · ⚙️ What the machine requires**

#### 8.2.1 · The template is the deliverable
(adding a page means copying it, not remembering the shape)
`ref/page-template.md` carries every recognized `##` section with one guide line each, so the shape travels with the file instead of living in someone's head.

#### 8.2.2 · A repeated `##` heading silently discards the first block
(the render keeps the LAST one, and nothing reports the loss)
`split_sections` builds a dict, so a second `## Where we are` wins and everything under the first never reaches the page.
This is the worst failure shape on the board: the file looks whole in the editor, the page is merely short, and no reader can tell that something is missing because nothing renders where it would have been.
QB2a carried two for two days, and roughly 4.6 KB of dated post-mortems under the first had never been seen by anyone. A fresh-context agent found it on 260802; no check did.
`check.py` now reports it as an ERROR rather than a warning, because it is silent data loss rather than a style defect. Its first run found two more pages on this board and one on another.

#### 8.2.3 · Section names must be kept verbatim
(`build.py` takes the WHOLE string after `## ` as the key)
`## Opening (required)` yields nothing at all, and nothing reports it, which is the worst kind of failure.
Required and optional markers go in the first body line, never in the heading.
Renaming a section goes through `ALIAS`, so boards already written keep parsing.

#### 8.2.4 · What every page requires
(seven base elements, plus one strongly advised section)
Required: title, `state`, `owner`, `## Opening`, `## Writing Style`, `## Aims`, `## States`.
Strongly advised: `## Files`.
A stage page also requires `## Stage Contract` and `## Content`, and declares explicit `requires`, `style-from` and `provides` rather than inferring a dependency from the order pages happen to appear in.
Fold order is fixed by `build.py`, and Log runs newest first.

### 9 · Evaluation
**One requirement stack**: how each authored unit is judged, and by whom.

```
📜 RESOLVE THE REQUIREMENTS ONCE

base page contract
       │ refines
       ▼
page kind / consumer variant
       │ refines
       ▼
page Writing Style + S Stage Contract
       │ refines
       ▼
local ### part purpose + #### job line
       │
       ├──▶ ⚙️ check.py           mechanics
       ├──▶ ✅ Quality Check      section-by-section iteration
       └──▶ 🧪 fresh reviewer     independent final gate

📋 ONE ROW PER UNIT
unit · requirements + source · verdict · evidence · smallest fix
```
🧪 Establishes how every section, Content part, and locally specified paragraph job is evaluated without duplicating the page requirements into another skill.

```
🧪 Evaluation · answers: does each authored unit meet its declared requirements?
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
👁 conveys   which requirement applies, whether it is met, the evidence, and the repair
📦 holds     requirement-resolution order · four axes · four verdicts
             one row per section or Content unit · one page-level verdict
✍️ source    haipipe-board-page owns the rubric · check.py supplies mechanics
             Quality Check runs it quickly · the fresh reviewer gates it
⚖️ rules     no duplicated evaluation skill · every MEETS names evidence
             conflicts are reported, never silently resolved · review is read-only
⏭ omit      never from the page contract · a page may run it whenever needed
```

**9.1 · 🧪 What an evaluation is**

#### 9.1.1 · Evaluation is comparison, not taste
(the reviewer compares what was written against what was required)
"This feels good" is not a verdict, because nobody else can reproduce it.
Every judgment names the requirement that applies, where that requirement comes from, the visible evidence, and the smallest repair when the evidence falls short.

#### 9.1.2 · Four sets of rules apply, in a fixed order
(each one may sharpen the one above it, and a clash is reported, not guessed)
The base section contract comes first, then the page kind or consumer variant.
Then the page's own `## Writing Style`, and on a stage page its Stage Contract.
Most specific of all: the `###` part's own purpose and the `####` job line under a heading.
A narrower rule may sharpen a broader one. A contradiction is reported as a conflict and waits for whoever owns it.

**9.2 · 📐 What gets judged**

#### 9.2.1 · Review units follow what was actually written
(every section present, every Content part, every paragraph with a declared job)
Each `##` section is tested against the reader question and the elements assigned to it.
Each `###` Content part is tested for its own purpose, whether it stands alone, whether it is in the right place, and the page-local rules.
Each `####` paragraph is tested against its heading and the `(job line)` under it, when one is declared.

#### 9.2.2 · Four axes, because they fail for different reasons
(mechanics, function, evidence, readability)
Mechanics asks whether the shape, order, ids and required pieces are structurally consistent.
Function asks whether the unit does the job its contract gives it.
Evidence asks whether a claim of compliance points at visible text, a State row, or a linked artifact.
Readability asks whether a reader who does NOT read English well can follow it (JL 260801).
That last one is harder than a zero-background test, and it catches what the other three miss: a paragraph can be structurally correct, do its job, cite real evidence, and still be unreadable because it is written in this board's own shorthand.
Run it heading first, since a heading is what a reader meets before deciding to read on. `The opening paragraph ends at the first blank line` passed every other axis and told a reader nothing; `A blank line decides what people see` says the same thing in words anyone has.

#### 9.2.3 · Four verdicts, and they are exhaustive
(MEETS, NEEDS WORK, N/A, NOT VERIFIABLE)
MEETS requires visible evidence, named.
NEEDS WORK requires a concrete defect and the smallest repair.
N/A means the requirement genuinely does not apply to this unit.
NOT VERIFIABLE means the evidence is unavailable, and it never counts as a pass.

**9.3 · 📋 What comes out of it**

#### 9.3.1 · One row keeps the judgment repairable
(unit, requirement and its source, verdict, evidence, smallest fix, all in one line)
The report writes one row per unit, so an author moves from a failed criterion straight to the edit that fixes it.
Requirement conflicts and mechanical findings follow the rows, and then one page-level verdict says whether another review is needed.

#### 9.3.2 · The tools that already exist are the execution path
(quick iteration and independent acceptance are two moments of the same review)
`cli/check.py --strict` supplies the deterministic half, and never claims to understand prose.
`cli/check.py --summary` turns those findings into a SCORE: how many of each rule, the worst pages, and how many pages are clean. That is what tells you whether the board is improving, which a list of 291 rows does not.
The page's `✅ Quality Check` reads the canonical contract and returns the row-by-row report while drafting.
After a revision, `haipipe-board-reviewer-agent` runs the same contract in a fresh context as the independent gate.
Together the checker and the fresh page review are QF1; QF2 separately tests whether a newly revised skill is usable from a fresh context.

#### 9.3.3 · No evaluation skill: the reviewer LOADS the contract
(a second copy of the rules is a second thing that can drift, and it drifts silently)
A skill whose job is evaluation would restate these requirements, and then there would be two sources for one rule.
So the judge is an AGENT that loads `haipipe-board-page` and `ref/writing-rules.md` at run time and holds no rules of its own.
That is what lets a rule written tonight be enforced by a reviewer written weeks ago, with no edit to the reviewer at all.
What DOES rot in an agent is its paths: `haipipe-board-reviewer-agent` was still calling `check.py` at the location it had before the CLI moved into `cli/`, which is the same failure `### 6.3.1` found in Files.
The repair pairs with `haipipe-writing`, ruled on QC1b: this judges, that repairs, which is the creator-and-reviewer split the task and discovery families already run.

#### 9.3.4 · A review never changes what the page says
(the evaluator reports; the author repairs and writes back)
Neither review surface edits prose, changes a State, ticks a decision, or closes a page.
The author applies the repair, updates the evidence and the State when that is justified, rebuilds, and runs the evaluation again.

**9.4 · 🔁 The other direction: evaluating the spec's own consumers**

#### 9.4.1 · A rule lives in more than one file, and the copies rot
(this page judges a page against the rules; something has to judge the rules' own carriers)
Everything above asks whether one page obeys the contract. This asks the opposite question: does everything that CARRIES the contract still state it correctly?
`### 6.1.1` is why the question exists. A rule stated here is written into `ref/page-template.md` so new pages inherit it, into `haipipe-board-page` so a reviewer can apply it, into the renderer that makes it visible, and into `check.py` that catches a page breaking it.
Every one of those is a file that can fall behind, and nothing on this page reports it.
On 260802 an audit found `haipipe-board-page` teaching the previous night's status glyphs in four places, and `haipipe-board-reviewer-agent` calling `check.py` at the path it had before the CLI moved into `cli/`.
Both had been correct when written. That is the point: a carrier does not break, it is simply left behind, and being left behind looks exactly like being right.

#### 9.4.2 · Hand the auditor the PAGE, never a summary of it
(a brief that restates the rules is itself a copy, and copies are the thing being audited)
Run the audit in a FRESH context, as an agent, and give it this page's path.
Do not paste the rules into the brief. A brief that restates them creates one more copy to keep in sync, which is the exact defect the audit exists to find.
Name the files in scope, name the traps, and let it read `## Law` for the rules.
The traps are worth naming because they are not obvious and each one cost real damage here: two vocabularies share glyphs and mean different things, a quoted sentence and a `## Log` line keep their original wording because they are history, and a find-and-replace across a family corrupts more than it fixes.
Its report takes the same shape as any other evaluation: one row per file, the stale statement quoted, and the smallest fix.
It must also report any contradiction it finds INSIDE this page, because a defect in the standard is worth more than any fix downstream of it.

### 10 · Using this page
**Two verbs**: what you say to make a new page, and to bring an existing one up to this.

```
🚪 ONE DOOR · say it to haipipe-board · page work routes to haipipe-board-page

📄 "create a new page on <topic>"     🔧 "working on <page>"
   📋 copies ref/page-template.md        ⚙️ runs the checker on that page
   ✍️ writes the seven sections          🔨 fixes the MECHANICAL findings
   🗂 registers it in board.md            👁 then reads for what no check sees
        └──────────────┬──────────────────────┘
                       ▼
        ⚙️ build ▸ 🧪 check ▸ ✅ zero findings, read on the RENDER

🚫 you never pick the sub-skill, and never call build.py or check.py
🚫 not done when the source is right: a dead watcher and a shut <details>
   each produced a correct file and a wrong page
```
📘 Establishes what a reader DOES with everything above: two things to say, and what finished means.

#### 10.1 · There is one door, and it is a sentence
(say it to `haipipe-board`; page work routes itself)
Say `create a new page on <topic>` or `update <page>` and you are done choosing. `haipipe-board` is the door for everything about a board, and anything about ONE PAGE routes to `haipipe-board-page`, which owns this contract and drives that page end to end (JL 260802).
You never pick the sub-skill, and you never call `build.py` or `check.py`. Someone asking for one page should not have to know which skill or which script does what.
Routing is by SCOPE, not by wording: one page belongs to the page skill, the board and its structure belong to the board skill, and a request naming a page id or a path is the page skill's even when it sounds structural, because whoever asks is looking at one page.
`haipipe-board` still owns the machinery. The page skill CALLS the engine rather than containing it, which is the same separation `### 9.3.3` requires of the reviewer: one source of rules, several things that run them.

#### 10.2 · Start from the findings, never from the top of the file
(every finding already names the rule it breaks and the part it is in)
That is what makes bringing an old page up cheap: the mechanical two thirds need no reading at all.
Dead `## Files` paths, a part with no figure, a figure with no caption, a group name that drifted from its Content part. On this board those are 66% of everything.
Only then read the page, for the half no checker reaches: the weak-English axis in `### 9`, whether each part still answers one question, and whether the Opening's visible paragraph says anything the title did not.

#### 10.3 · Finished means zero findings on that page, read on the render
(`--summary` scores it; the page is the proof, not the file)
`--summary` gives findings per rule, the worst pages, and how many are clean, so "is this better" is a number rather than an impression.
A page at zero is what the others get made to look like, which is the whole reason this page exists.
Then open the rendered page and read what a reader opens. Source-is-correct is not page-is-correct: a stopped watcher and a shut `<details>` each produced a correct file and a wrong page on 260801.

#### 10.4 · The rule travels with the fix
(a repair that stops at the page will be needed again next week)
When a fix reveals a rule that was never written down, write it in three places: here, in `ref/page-template.md` so new pages inherit it, and in `haipipe-board-page` so the verbs apply it.
That is `### 6.1.1`'s question in reverse, and it is what turns one page's repair into every page's default.

## Law
- 🖌 **A page shows no canvas link rather than one that 404s**: JL 260802, ruling B. Four pages pointed at frames nobody had drawn, so each promised a picture and delivered an error. The link goes, and a sentence says which frame is missing and that the link returns when it is drawn.
- 🗺 **Design history follows the SUBJECT, not the page that once held it**: JL 260802, ruling A. `_archive/QB4b` §1-§4 answered "how does the canvas work", so it moved to `QB8`, which owns attaching a drawing, rather than back into the page-template contract.
- 🧹 **One vocabulary across every board, swept in one pass**: JL 260802, ruling A. The alternative was sweeping each board when next touched, which leaves the checker reporting a rule most boards do not yet follow.
- 🗣 **Do not reopen what the sentence already settled**: `"let's make this to be 'Folds', why 'fold', very bad"` is one instruction, not an instruction plus an objection. CC split it, took the first half as a rename and the second as an unresolved complaint about the word, and raised a Decision Now row recommending a name JL had never mentioned.
  A row that re-asks a question already answered costs more than a missing row: it sits in `Decision Now` looking like real work, and the person has to spend a turn discovering they already decided it.
  When a quote is the evidence for a row, read the whole quote first, and quote it in the row.
- 🕰️ **A rename the renderer forgives is a rename nobody finishes**: when `src/common.py` aliases a retired section name, every page on the old name keeps rendering correctly, so the drift is invisible and nobody is ever prompted to finish it.
  On 260802 that cost 45 of 55 pages, still on names retired 260731, while `haipipe-board-page` stated in writing that the conversion was done.
  Retire a name in the CHECKER on the day you retire it in the contract; the alias exists so old pages still render, not so new pages may use it.
- 📄 **One heading, once**: a repeated `## ` heading silently discards everything under the first, because `split_sections` builds a dict and the later block wins (JL 260802, found by a fresh-context agent).
  It is the worst failure shape on the board: the file looks whole in the editor, the page is merely short, and nothing renders where the lost block would have been, so no reader can tell.
  QB2a held two `## Where we are` for two days with 4.6 KB of dated records under the first that nobody had ever seen. `check.py` reports it as an ERROR, and its first run found two more pages here and one on another board.
- 🗣 **Decision on the page**: a decision is a row in the owning page's `States › Decision Now`, never a question asked only in a chat session (JL 260802).
  Write the row first, then say in chat which page and which row; the chat line is a pointer and the page is the decision.
  A decision left in a session cannot be seen by anyone else, carries no `Blocks:` and no `Default:` so it evaporates instead of resolving itself, and leaves no record of the options weighed, which is what the next person to reopen it needs.
  JL ruled it after an evening in which several proposals were put to him in chat and only one of them, the `haipipe-writing` roster row on QC1b, was ever written down.
- 📎 **Files**: the action map for CHANGING what the page rules (JL 260802)
  The question it answers is "if I change a rule here, what do I touch?", not "which files relate to this topic", and those are different lists of very different length.
  Group by what you DO to a file rather than by what it is, since the same file is an input on one page and an output on another; the trio Engines, Inputs, Outputs is a starting point and not a schema, because `### 0.5`'s contextual-naming rule covers Files too.
  A path is either right or a dead end, and the reader finds out only after following it, so `check.py` resolves every backticked path in the section.
  This page carried four dead paths by 260802 and a whole group that had outlived the Content part it served, which is why the check exists rather than a rule asking people to be careful.
- 🔗 **Heading chip**: a short id and COPIES the full address (JL 260801)
  The two jobs pull opposite ways: a reader wants one token they can see at a glance and say out loud, while Claude Code needs `QB4 / Content / 0 · The page protocol · <file>` to open the right place.
  One string cannot serve both, so the label shrank and the clipboard payload did not.
  A Content division reuses the `C1` its own sentence grammar already assigns rather than minting a second id for the same thing, and every other heading drops the page id off the front, since the browser tab and the breadcrumb both already say which page this is.
- 🪟 **Tab title**: the page id comes first, so a dozen open board tabs tell each other apart (JL 260801)
  The tab reads `QB4 · The page template: one grammar every page kind obeys`, because a tab shows perhaps twenty characters and a dozen open board tabs all begin with the same kind of phrase.
  The id is the one token that tells them apart, and it is what a person says out loud when naming a page.
- ⚙️ **Finished**: a change is done when it is ON THE RENDERED PAGE, and nobody is asked for permission on the way (JL 260801)
  JL: "Dont wait me to say go next time. Just go ahead and don't stop until the content is updated in the Page."
  So the unit of work is not an edit, it is a visible page: write the source, propagate the rule to `ref/page-template.md` and `haipipe-board-page` so a new page inherits it, run the checker, and confirm the RENDER rather than the markdown.
  Stopping to ask for a go in the middle leaves the change half-applied, which is the state this page spent an evening proving is worse than either finishing or not starting: a renamed label with fourteen sentences still naming the old one, a rule written on one page and in no template.
  Verification is on the artifact a reader opens: this session twice reported work as landed when the source was correct and the page was not, once because the watcher was dead and once because the text was present but sitting inside a shut section.
- 📄 **Template file**: it is `ref/page-template.md`, named for what it templates rather than for one page kind (JL 260801)
  It was `ref/q-template.md`, from the era when Q was the only page kind, and it has served all three kinds for months: its own instructions tell you to copy it for a decision page OR a lifecycle-stage page.
  The name therefore failed the same test this page's own title failed that morning, which is that a name says what the thing is FOR rather than which era it came from.
  The rename swept 101 references across 30 files and deliberately left 69 on the old name, because those sit in Log and CHANGELOG lines that record what was true when they were written.
- 🏷 **Title**: the page's purpose, in sentence case (JL 260801)
  Capitalize the first word and proper nouns and nothing else, and let a colon carry a short subtitle, which is usually where the purpose lands.
  The substance half is the half that matters: a title naming only its topic makes the reader open the page to find out what the page was for, and on the Index the title is the only line they get before choosing.
  Sentence case was already the practice on 44 of this board's 46 pages and was written down nowhere, so `Page Template design` could carry `Page Template` from the Title Case era and a freshly typed `design` from the sentence-case one through a rewording, with nothing to catch the seam.
  The rule now sits here, in `### 8`, in `## Writing Style`, in `ref/page-template.md`'s first line, and in `haipipe-board-page`, so a new page inherits it from the template rather than from whichever neighbour it was copied off.
- 📜 **Stage Contract**: a stage's obligations live under ONE name, and that name is Stage Contract (JL 260801)
  Stage Record is retired and Stage Protocol is never introduced.
  Two names for one obligation meant no writer could say which held what, and the page could not answer the question either.
  A legacy `### Stage Record` under Content still renders, lifted into the contract as its opening lines, because deleting someone else's writing on read is a silent loss.
- 📜 **On-stage order**: fixed, and it never moves: intent first, status second
  Q uses `Opening → Diagram → Content → Aims → States → Files`.
  S carries `Stage Contract` inside Opening (collapsed), then follows the same sequence.
  Opening contains the question lead, scope, and stakes; S still requires explicit Content for its own substance.
- 🎯 **Aims and States**: the paired plural section labels (JL 260801)
  `Aims` contains Aim records; `States` contains their matching State records.
  The plural labels keep the two collections visually consistent, while one Aim still maps to exactly one current State.
  Singular `State`, `Where we are`, and `Now` remain compatibility aliases for old sources; every new source writes `## States`.
- ⚖️ **Aim vs State**: durable intent; its State is present fact (JL 260801)
  Every Aim has a stable A/P id and a testable Done when; optional Plan text stays subordinate to that Aim.
  Every Aim has exactly one current State row, and the group/id correspondence makes Content, Aim, and State comparable without forcing one Aim per division.
  Aims never carry checkboxes; States use ⬜, 🔨, 🧠, ✅, or ❄️, and status transitions are recorded in Log.
- 🧬 **Page kinds**: grammar, two workflow kinds
  Q is a decision and closes by settlement; S is a lifecycle stage and closes at its human gate.
  They share the renderer and reading order, not their counters or governance.
- 🧭 **Opening first**: orientation always precedes substance, so Opening comes before Content
  The page must orient a zero-background reader before presenting substantive material.
  Opening carries the actual question lead and its scope.
  More details sits in Opening's drawer on Q and S alike (JL 260729; until then Q carried it under Content); S adds the Stage Contract, all collapsed.
  An optional collapsed Diagram row separates Opening from the remaining Content.
- 🧭 **No Structure row**: dropped from Opening (JL 260801)
  The left panel index already carries the page's map, so rendering a generated Structure row inside Opening duplicates it.
  Opening's drawer therefore holds More details and Writing Style, plus the S contract when supplied.
- ⚖️ **Writing Style**: a page element, and it is required (JL 260801)
  Every page declares how it must be written, in its own `## Writing Style` section, rendered as a plain row in Opening's drawer beside More details.
  It is not a property of one workflow: it was reached through S's Stage Contract, but a page that cannot say how it should be written cannot be edited consistently by the next person, whatever kind it is.
  Where the rules come from may differ: a stage composes them from its templates, an ordinary page writes them itself.
  The section is the same section on every page.
  It states rules for editing THIS page. Whether the resulting prose satisfies the resolved contract is the fresh reviewer's verdict inside the per-change `QF1` gate, not this section's own claim.
- 🧭 **The door**: lead question is the door, and Opening itself never folds (JL 260725)
  The 🧭 Opening heading and the visible paragraph are always on stage; clicking the paragraph opens More details, Writing Style and, on S, the Stage Contract.
  A version that hung the fold on the section name instead was reverted the same day: a shut row reading only "🧭 Opening" gives the reader nothing to decide with, and the 260724 Law about invisible folds applies to a section heading exactly as it applies to an item.
  Put the handle on the thing a reader wants explained, which is the question itself, not on the label above it.
  One door, and behind it everything is flat: no row inside the drawer folds again, because a second layer of ▸ makes the material read as missing rather than as filed, which is exactly how JL described it.
  The drawer's headings carry no icons, because two of the seven did and five did not, and partial decoration reads as a type distinction that is not there.
  The lead keeps its original markup, a `<p class="qlead">` inside the summary, so the serif page and the 18px/21px sizing keep matching; moving that class onto the summary silently dropped both rules.
- 🎯 **S consumers**: they become Content-linked Aims rather than a list of their own
  Keep the Aim id, Q id, Description, Reason, Probe, and Done when together inside one Aim record.
  Mark the matching State row ✅ only after answer, interpretation, and Content integration; summarize rather than duplicate in States.
- 📚 **S Content**: it holds the stage's real product only (JL 260725)
  Content is the thing the stage makes, so on a manuscript page it is the section itself and nothing else.
  Required Inputs, Venue, and upstream acceptance conditions belong to Stage Contract inside Opening; page prose rules belong to `## Writing Style`; current flags and corrections belong to States; intended outcomes belong to Aims.
  The Content heading names the stage (`📚 Content · Main 7 §6 Results`) instead of counting subsections, because a name is a test the page can fail and a count is not.
- 📚 **Two heading levels**: Content carries exactly two, and the NUMBER carries the depth (JL 260725)
  Every division that holds content of its own is a direct `###` and folds on its own; everything one step inside it is `####`, with no exceptions.
  Depth is read off the numbering, `§6` against `§6.1`, rather than off the heading level, because the board folds exactly one level and a third level would cost per-subsection folding on the longest pages.
  A division is emitted only when it holds prose of its own, so a flat section carries one `### §1 Introduction` over its paragraphs and a subsectioned one goes straight to `### §6.1`, and no page opens a box onto nothing.
  This makes the shape checkable without reading the prose: the subsection count is the number of `###` headings whose number contains a dot.
- 📍 **Decision Now**: FIRST in States, and a row must earn its place (JL 260802)
  States is a report; Decision Now is the one part of it that asks the reader to do something, so it goes above the status rows rather than under nine groups of them.
  A row earns its place only when something STOPS until it is answered. That test exists because this page's own record shows the opposite habit: about 46 decisions were made on 260801 and 260802 and written into Log, none of them through Decision Now, while the two rows that were in Decision Now had sat unanswered since 260801.
  A decision that matters gets made in conversation within minutes, so this list accumulates the residue rather than the substance, and the cause is structural: decisions are proposed by an agent, and an agent can generate a plausible fork about anything.
  So every row carries `Blocks:`, and a row that blocks nothing must carry `Default:` naming the option that takes effect if nobody answers. The ruling then goes to Log as CC's and can be overturned like any other.
  A blocking row has no default, because guessing there produces work that has to be thrown away, which is the only thing worth stopping for.
  The row's shape is fixed and every field is marked: 🗣 the question as the row's own title, 📍 `Part`, 🔔 `Why now`, the options `A ·` `B ·` … with ⭐ on the one CC recommends, 🛑 `Blocks`, and 🤖 `If nobody answers`.
  The emoji are not decoration: nine identical `Label:` rows gave the eye nothing to sort the question from the options from the consequence (JL 260802).
  The star sits on the option rather than on a line at the bottom repeating a letter, which removes a row and lets a reader find the answer without reading to the end.
  Give a decision as many options as it actually has, never three by habit: a filler option is worse than none, because the reader must read it before discovering it was filler.
- 🎯 **Group ids**: Aims or States group is `A<n>`, and takes its Content part's number, name, and emoji (JL 260801, letter fixed 260802)
  `### 3 · 📚 Content` is answered by `### A3 · 📚 Content` in Aims and again in States, so intent, fact, and substance line up by number, by name, and by eye.
  The letter is `A` because the group holds A-records: `C3` written over a list of `A3.1` rows made a reader translate one letter into another for no gain. `C<n>` still resolves, since older boards are full of it.
  JL asked whether they could share names after reading `### 0.5`; five of this page's six groups already did, which is why the sixth had gone unnoticed.
  That sixth was `### C1 · The base and its variants`, still named for a topic `### 1` stopped carrying when it became Opening, and its Aim actually belonged to `### 3`, where `3.11` owns page kinds.
  Nothing reported it because nothing compared the two lists; `check.py` now does.
  `P` is the exception, since a page-level target belongs to no single division.
- 📚 **Numbering**: content is numbered all the way down (JL 260801)
  A division is `### 3 · Content`, a group inside it is `**3.2 · Group title**`, and a paragraph is `#### 3.2.1 · Its heading`; an ungrouped division numbers its paragraphs straight through as `#### 3.1 ·`.
  The depth of the number therefore says whether a group exists, which is the rule the board already applies one level up with `§6` against `§6.1`.
  JL asked for it of `### 1` and it paid immediately: numbering exposed a group holding exactly ONE paragraph, which is the floating-group-title defect `### 3` forbids and which no one had seen while the headings were unnumbered.
  It also gives every paragraph a name a person can say in chat, since `1.2.3` can be pointed at and a heading cannot.
- 🏷 **Group title**: lead a run of `####` paragraphs (JL 260801)
  A whole-line `**bold**` must lead a real group, and a run of paragraph headings is one; only a bold line floating above a single paragraph is the defect the rule was made for.
  It matters once a division gets long: `### 1` reached eleven `####` while the rules for the Opening were being written, and eleven flat paragraph headings is a list a reader scrolls rather than a structure they can hold.
  Four group titles now carve it into what the Opening owes, the paragraph on stage, what that paragraph must accomplish, and the drawer behind it, which is the shape `### 3` already permits.
  `check.py` accepted only `- item` runs until this ruling, because that was the only kind of run in the grammar when the check was written.
- 🏷 **Paragraph heading**: not a group title (JL 260725)
  `####` is its own level with no icon; a full-line `**bold**` is a group title and keeps 🔹.
  Flattening `####` to bold made every paragraph claim to lead a run of items, which is a false statement about the page rather than surplus decoration.
  A full-line `(…)` immediately under a paragraph heading is that paragraph's job and stays on stage in grey italic, because a scan hook behind a click cannot be scanned.
- 🖼 **Diagram halves**: as ▧ ASCII open over ✏️ Excalidraw shut (JL 260726)
  The two halves are ranked, not paired: the figure is what a reader came for, and the canvas is where colleagues draw together, so equal weight would misdescribe them.
  The split is a RENDER decision and the source stays one plain `## Diagram`, which is why no page had to be migrated and a page that later gains a canvas splits itself.
  A bare Excalidraw URL alone on a line is the canvas and every other line is the figure; a URL inside a fence stays in the figure, where its author drew it.
  The canvas row is emitted even when empty because the 🖌 attach button lives there, and scriptless it still says truthfully that no canvas is attached.
- 🖼 **Copyable figure**: an ASCII figure must survive being copied out of the page
  Copying a page into chat or an email is a use the board is for, so a figure whose meaning lives in whitespace is broken by its own purpose.
  Never draw two trees side by side: the column boundary disappears on paste and the right column's rows read as branches of the left one.
- 📚 **S Content authorship**: composed at creation by its consumer, and owned by the page afterwards (JL 260725)
  The consumer creator resolves the stage template, venue overlay, and previous contracts, then writes the stage-specific result as direct `###` headings with guide text.
  Board `stage.py` creates the generic page and syncs only the managed Stage Contract and Writing Style inheritance blocks.
  Render never regenerates authored Content; changes to explicit `requires` or `style-from` sources surface through the contract digest instead of a silent rewrite.
- 🧭 **Opening paragraph**: what fits before the first blank line, and it is small (JL 260801)
  `build.py` splits `## Opening` on its first blank line: above it is the one paragraph a reader sees, joined into a single block; below it is the `More details` drawer, behind a click.
  That makes the blank line the only control a writer has over what is visible, and nothing warns you when it sits in the wrong place: this page spent a round rendering one bare question while five explaining sentences sat unread in the drawer.
  The paragraph is 4 to 5 sentences, about five lines on screen, under roughly 450 characters, measured on the RENDER rather than the source, since one sentence per source line merges into one block.
  It is written in plain English for a reader whose English is weak, because a shorter common word always beats a precise rare one: `settles a decision`, not `argues one choice to a close`.
  Its shape is the question, then what the question's own words mean, then why that is hard, then what this page decides.
- 🧭 **No growing roster**: Opening never opens with a list that will grow (JL 260801)
  Naming today's members reads as though the set were closed, and it makes the Opening a maintenance obligation that every new member reopens.
  JL caught it on this page, whose Opening listed the three page kinds and would have needed a fourth line the day a fourth kind arrived: what happens when we have more page kinds, he asked, do we just keep writing here?
  So name examples and say the set grows, and keep the roster itself in the Content division that owns it, which is where a new member gets written down.
- 🖼 **Figure caption**: every figure carries one line above it saying what it shows (JL 260801)
  Write `**Name**: what this diagram shows.` on the line directly above the fence, and keep it to one line.
  JL asked for it after noticing that a section may hold more than one figure: this page's `## Diagram` carried two fences and labelled neither, so a reader had to decode each one before learning what it was.
  The caption goes ABOVE rather than below, because an explanation that arrives after the figure arrives after the reader has already started working it out.
- ⚖️ **Example pair**: a good/bad pair gets its own line, marked ✅ and ❌ (JL 260801)
  Never bury a contrast inside a sentence: `Prefer X to Y, and Z to W` makes the reader hold four quoted phrases at once and work out which two are the good ones, which is the work the example existed to save.
  JL asked for it of this page's own plain-English paragraph, where two swaps were written as one prose sentence and neither was findable.
  The `✅`/`❌` pair is already the board's vocabulary inside figures, so putting it in prose is not a new convention, it is the same one reaching the place it was needed most.
- 🖼 **Figure row**: a label and its value, never a clause (JL 260801)
  If a row could end in a period it is prose, and it belongs in the paragraph under the figure rather than inside the fence.
  JL asked why the figures carried so many words; the answer was that this page's own two `## Diagram` fences put a full explanatory sentence on every row, ran 43 and 32 lines, and had to be READ rather than seen.
  Redrawing them to labels and values cut 75 lines to 38 and roughly 530 words to 180, and nothing was lost, because every removed sentence was already written in the prose below the figure.
- ⚖️ **More details**: a list of labelled parts, never one block of prose (JL 260801)
  Each part starts with a bold label saying what it answers, then its sentences, with a blank line between parts.
  The two halves of an Opening have two different readers: the paragraph on stage is read straight through by someone deciding whether to stay, and the drawer is opened by someone who has already decided and is looking for one specific thing.
  Merging the drawer's source lines into paragraphs (260801) fixed one failure and created this one, by turning a column of fragments into a single block with no way in except the top; the bold leads are what give it a way in.
- 🧭 **Opening vocabulary**: defines the words its own question uses (JL 260801)
  When the lead question names this board's own things, the rationale's first job is to make each of those terms concrete, one line each, with a real example rather than a restatement.
  JL asked for it after reading this page's own question, which fires `decision`, `lifecycle-stage`, and `skill-definition` at a reader who has been given no way to know what any of them is: the question is sharp precisely because it is specific, and being specific is what made it unreadable cold.
  The trap is that a restatement passes the author's eye as an explanation, because the author already knows what the words mean; the test is whether a real example follows, since an example cannot be written without saying the thing.
  This rule sits ABOVE the bearing rule below it, because a reader who cannot parse the question cannot yet be told where the page sits or why it matters.
  The names chosen in the question then bind the rest of the page: the three kinds are named identically in the Opening, in `### 3`, and here, since a kind answering to two names cannot be looked up.
- 🧭 **Opening bearing**: places the page before it argues for it (JL 260801)
  The rationale opens by naming the neighbouring page the reader most likely came from and what that page handles, then says what this page takes up from there.
  JL asked for it after reading an Opening that argued its case correctly to a reader who already knew the board: introduce the previous page, so that even a newcomer reading this one gets the sense of what the place is doing.
  Two or three sentences carry the bearing, and naming the page that continues the subject is worth one more, because it tells the reader where to stop expecting.
  This is why the rule sits above the stake rather than beside it: a reader who cannot locate the page cannot judge the case for it.
- 📑 **Folds**: fifth section is `Folds`, capitalized like its siblings (JL 260801)
  Opening, Diagram, Content, Aims, States, Files and Folds are all section names, so writing one of them in lower case made it read as a mechanism rather than as a place on the page.
  The word itself is still under objection (JL 260801: "why 'fold', very bad") and the successor sits in Decision Now.
  Lower-case "fold" survives only where it means the widget: a shut fold, a section that folds, an apparatus folded under its sentence.
- 🧭 **Opening shape**: one question lead, then one rationale paragraph
  The lead is the actual prompt in Opening.
  The paragraph makes the page-specific stake clear in its own natural order; difficulty, failure, and downstream effect are diagnostic questions rather than required sentence slots.
  It renders as "More details" inside Opening on Q and S alike (JL 260729; the row carried the label "Why this matters" until JL renamed it on 260801).
- ✍️ **Item explanation**: a paragraph, not a clause (JL 260724)
  The `- short heading` + indented explanation pattern applies to list records, Aims, and States, and the explanation is a real paragraph: what it means, what happened, what we understand so far, and why it ended up this way.
  Opening itself uses the lead-plus-rationale shape above, not item bullets.
- ✏️ **Sentence apparatus**: shut by default, and its badge says which KIND is underneath (JL 260801)
  Nothing on a page opens itself any more, a person's comment included; the badge hanging at the sentence end is what keeps the 260724 rule that an invisible fold is a broken fold.
  The badge earns that only because it names the kind: `💬 2` means two people are waiting on an answer, `✎ 2` means two edits are recorded, and `⚑ 2` means two typed lanes are filed.
  A person waiting outranks a record, so a sentence carrying both shows `💬`.
  The three were one glyph until 260801, and then `💬` for a while, which said someone was owed an answer when the row was only an edit note.
  Before this the count was the same glyph either way, so a reader could see that something was attached and not whether it was owed to anyone, which is the one thing worth auto-opening for.
  A comment used to burst its sentence open for exactly that reason, and once every section shut it became the only loud thing on a calm page.
- 🗃 **Visible fold control**: foldable items carry a visible `[more details]` control (JL 260724)
  Any item with an explanation renders its heading followed by a small `[more details]` pill, which reads `hide` once open.
  Before this, the only cue that an item could be opened was a small `▸` triangle, and readers simply did not know there was anything beneath; the fold was working and invisible at the same time.
  Implemented purely in CSS via `::after`, so the zero-script invariant holds: strip every script and the paragraphs are still in the DOM and still open on click.
- ⚖️ **Focus mode**: = a slide, not a card
  Strip border, corners, background; content flows directly; `min-height` fills the screen.
  Title 38px, lead 21px, body 16px, width 1000px.
  Pure CSS (`:target` + `:has()`).
- 🗃 **Stage and fold**: what is on stage, what folds
  On stage: title; compass Opening with the question lead; the Diagram, Content, Aims, States, and Files headings.

## Lesson
- 🪤 **Template meta parser**: in the usage comment of the template, never start a line with `state:` / `owner:` / `method:`
  The meta parser swallows any line whose first word is one of these; the first draft corrupted the status exactly this way; writing `· state …` dodges it.
- 🧹 **Stale template prose**: self-contradictions in the template must be purged
  The template still said "newest Log lines at the bottom" long after reverse order was settled, exactly the kind of stale sentence a zero-background reader spots first.
- 🔄 **Settled can be overturned**: a page closed with every line ticked was reopened by one remark
  This question was closed ✅ on 260723 with every finish line ticked, and was knocked back to 🟡 the same day by a single remark from JL: a zero-background reader could hardly understand the page.
  Nothing about the work was wrong; the checklist was.
  It contained eight conditions about structure and not one about the person the structure exists for, so it could be completed in full while the actual goal went unmet. **A checklist that omits the real user proves nothing when fully ticked**, and the failure is invisible from the inside, because the author always reads the page with the context already in their head.
- 🔀 **Order beats wording**: the same sentences reordered read as a different document
  The same sentences, with `Where we are` above the goal versus below it, read like two different documents.
  Before the redesign the page opened with implementation detail; afterwards it opened with the question.
  No prose was rewritten in that pass and yet the page became readable.
  The general form: when a page is hard to follow, reorder before rewriting, because wording improvements applied to a bad order are largely wasted effort.
- 📦 **Folding is free**: long explanations are free when they fold
  Item explanations are collapsed by default while their headings stay visible, which changes the economics of writing them.
  A scanning reader pays nothing for a paragraph they do not open, so the usual pressure to compress an explanation into a clause disappears, and compressed clauses are exactly what strips out the reasoning and the history that make a decision reviewable later.
  The rule that follows: write the heading for the scanner and the paragraph for the person who stops.
- 🧩 **A new level inherits nothing**: a new body level inherits none of the fixes the old one accumulated
  The paragraph heading shipped with the same base spacing as the group title but without the patch that focus mode had needed, so `.q:target .ph` at (0,3,0) outranked `.ph:first-child` at (0,2,0) and the first paragraph after a section heading opened 22px below it instead of 2px.
  JL saw it as inconsistency rather than as a gap, which is the more useful description: it only appeared where a section began with a heading, so some sections read tight and others split open with no visible cause, and an inconsistency with no visible cause is what makes a page feel unreliable rather than merely imperfect.
  The same bug had already been found and patched once for group titles, and the patch was invisible to whoever added the next level.
  Both are now handled by one selector so they cannot drift apart again, and the general form is worth keeping: when a construct is split into two, every accumulated exception has to be split with it, because exceptions live where nobody looks for them.

## Glossary
- 🧬 **page kind**: a family of pages that hold the same sort of material, such as decision pages or lifecycle-stage pages. Every kind uses the same seven sections in the same order, and only Content changes between them (JL 260802: "what is 'A Kind', not good for the weaker English speaker").
- ⚑ **evidence card**: everything attached to one sentence, shown when the sentence is clicked. Three kinds hang there: a typed lane (⚑), a comment (💬), and a change record (✎). `QB5a` owns it.
- 👁 **on stage**: visible on the page without clicking anything. Everything else needs one click, and this page used the phrase seventeen times before it was ever explained (JL 260801: "what does the on stage mean here?").
- 📖 **More details**: the fold that holds everything written below `## Opening`'s first blank line. It was labelled "Why this matters" until 260801.
- 🖼 **unframed**: no border, rounded corners, or card background wrapping the content; it sits directly on the page.
- 🔹 **group title**: a full-line bold sentence in the body that leads a run of items; shown with 🔹 or the author's own emoji.
- 🗂 **division**: any Content part that folds on its own, written as a direct `###`; a manuscript subsection is one, and so is a flat section standing over its own paragraphs.
- 📄 **paragraph heading**: a `####` line naming one paragraph inside a division; it carries no icon and is one size below a group title, which is the distinction that was invisible while it rendered as bold.
- 🏷 **job line**: the full-line `(…)` directly under a paragraph heading, saying what that paragraph does; it stays on stage in grey italic as a scan hook.

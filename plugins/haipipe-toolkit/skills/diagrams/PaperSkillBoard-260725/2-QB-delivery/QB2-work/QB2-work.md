## Opening

After Opening, how does the paper grow the evidence it needs and put it in an order?

A bank is where evidence lives outside the paper, such as a task run that produced a coefficient. A probe binds one question the paper cannot answer to the bank answer that does. The arc is the order those answers are revealed in. The paper writes the need, the binding, and the order, and never the search or the computation.

**Where this page sits**: QB1 Opening hands over a promise nobody has proved yet, and QB3 Literature and QB4 Value describe what comes back, source by source and number by number.
This concern turns the promise into owned questions, and since 260802 it also holds the arc those answers form.

**Why the arc belongs here and not with the promise**: an arc is built from the claim ledger.
You cannot order an argument before you know which claims survived contact with the evidence, and QB1's own text already said a returned answer may reopen the arc.
JL ruled it into Work on 260802.

**What the split costs and buys**: it costs an extra hop for every fact, and it buys a paper where every claim has a traceable owner outside the prose.
The banks are shared across papers, so the hop is also what lets a second paper reuse the first paper's evidence instead of redoing it.

## Writing Style

How this page must be written. Read it before editing, and edit to it.

**Inherited from `QB4`**: the page grammar, the section order, and the sentence rules come from `../BoardSkillBoard-260722/QPs-page-structure/QPs1-overall/QPs1-overall.md` and are not restated here.

**Never describe how a bank does its work**: this page owns the boundary, the probe binding, and the arc, and nothing on the other side of it.
How discovery searches, or how a task computes, belongs to those layers; a sentence here that explains their internals has crossed the line this page exists to draw.

**Say "grows the bank", not "gets an answer"**: the phrasing carries the point that the bank is permanent and the paper is one consumer of it.
"Gets an answer" makes the bank sound like a service call and hides the reuse that justifies the whole arrangement.

**A Decision Now row is read once, by a person in a hurry**: short sentences, plain words, real numbers.
Any option a reader has to re-read has already failed, whatever it says.

**The arc is an ORDER, not prose**: narrative here means which claim is revealed when, and the section list that follows from it.
Wording belongs to QB6 Main and to section-edit; a sentence about phrasing has left this page.

#### 1.1 · An owned path is what separates a probe from a note
(a need with no owning bank entry is a wish, and it will still be a wish at submission)
The gate does not ask whether the answer is good, it asks whether the question has an owner.
A need recorded without a bound path looks identical to a satisfied one from inside the paper, which is exactly how a claim reaches a reviewer unsupported.

#### 1.2 · The arc is downstream of the answers, which is why it moved here
(ordering claims before knowing which survive produces an arc that has to be rebuilt)
A narrative is the reveal order of the claim ledger, so it can only be honest once the ledger is.
The old grouping put it beside the promise, where it read as something decided up front, and the page that owned it already admitted a returned answer could reopen it.

#### 1.3 · Three stages, two families, and one of them is not Work's
(narrative keeps `board_family: Venue` even though this concern now holds it)
Resource writes `S-Work-0-resources.md` and claims writes `S-Work-1-claims.md`, both family Work.
Narrative writes `S-Venue-2-narrative.md`, because JL ruled on 260725 that the narrative is the third half of the venue contract, and the 260802 move to this concern changed the Delivery grouping without changing the family.
So a reader looking for Work's third page will not find an `S-Work-2`, and this is the clearest case on the board of a Delivery concern and a board family deliberately not lining up.

#### 1.4 · The division COUNT is not fixed, and each stage proves it differently
(QB4 §3.2.2: one part is one piece of the thing the page makes, so the artifact decides)
Seed, venue, and pitch each happen to carry four sections, which makes four look like the shape.
It is not. Resource carries one division per RESOURCE, so the count is however many resources the paper has; claims carries two, the hypotheses and the ledger; narrative carries one per MANUSCRIPT SECTION plus a spine and a footer.
Three stages, three different division sets, and every one of them still mirrors into `A<n>` groups by number.

### 2 · Stage Resource

**What a resource page owes**: for each resource, does it exist, and can it carry the claim?

```text
  📦 STAGE RESOURCE · order 1a
  "Does what this paper needs EXIST, and can it CARRY the claim?"
  📄 S-Work-0-resources.md      🔨 draft · probe · revise · check

  📚 ## Content · ONE division PER RESOURCE   🎯 ## Aims · mirrored
  ────────────────────────────────────────    ──────────────────────
  ### 1 · Resource 1 · <name>          ━━▶    ### A1 · 📦 Resource 1
        ### <coverage & size>                       - A1.1 · Q-Resource-1
        ### <provenance / validation>
        ### Serves & carries
  ### 2 · Resource 2 · <name>          ━━▶    ### A2 · 📦 Resource 2
        ### <topic> · ### Serves & carries          - A2.1 · Q-Resource-2

  ⚡ the COUNT follows the paper, not the template
  🔑 every division ends on "Serves & carries": which claim it feeds,
     and whether it is strong enough to feed it
```

📦 Establishes that a resource page is a per-resource inventory, and what each entry must settle.

#### 2.1 · Two questions per resource, and the second is the one that fails
(existence is cheap to check; carrying capacity is the judgment)
Whether a dataset exists, and at what coverage and size, is a fact anyone can verify.
Whether it can carry the claim it is being asked to support is a different question, and it is the one that decides whether the paper is feasible at all.

#### 2.2 · Serves and carries is the link back to the claim
(a resource with no named claim is a resource nobody asked for)
Each division ends by naming which claim the resource feeds and whether it is strong enough to feed it.
That is what lets QB3 Literature and QB4 Value describe returned evidence without re-deriving why it was wanted.

### 3 · Stage Claims

**What a claims page owes**: venue-neutral hypotheses, and a ledger whose statuses are honest.

```text
  🎯 STAGE CLAIMS · order 1b
  "Which claims are supported, refuted, or inconclusive?"
  📄 S-Work-1-claims.md         🔨 draft · probe · revise · check

  📚 ## Content · two divisions        🎯 ## Aims · mirrored groups
  ─────────────────────────────        ──────────────────────────────
  ### 1 · Hypotheses           ━━▶     ### A1 · 🔬 Hypotheses
        H1 core · H2 boundary                - A1.1 · Q-Claim-1 · …
        H3 mechanism
        ⚠️ VENUE-NEUTRAL
  ### 2 · Claims               ━━▶     ### A2 · 📒 Claims
        C1 · C2 · C3, each with              - A2.1 · Q-Claim-2 · …
        a STATUS and its evidence            - A2.2 · Q-Claim-4 · …

  🏷 status vocabulary: supported · refuted · inconclusive
  🔗 a claim cites its questions inline: Evidence: [Q-Claim-1] [Q-Claim-2]
```

🎯 Establishes the ledger this concern is named for, and the one property that keeps it reusable across venues.

#### 3.1 · Hypotheses stay venue-neutral, and the reframing happens later
(the same H1 becomes RQ1 for a different outlet, and that is PITCH's job, not this page's)
A hypothesis written for one journal has to be rewritten for the next, and a ledger that needs rewriting on retarget is not a ledger.
Keeping H1, H2, and H3 neutral is what lets QB1's pitch stage designate one as `[primary]` for a venue without touching this page.

#### 3.2 · The status vocabulary is three words, and refuted is one of them
(a ledger that can only say supported is a marketing document)
Each claim carries `supported`, `refuted`, or `inconclusive`, and each is a legitimate resting state.
`inconclusive` is the one that keeps the ledger honest, because it is what a real answer often is and it has nowhere else to go.

#### 3.3 · A claim cites its questions inline, which is what makes the division its Aims group
(`Evidence: [Q-Claim-1] [Q-Claim-2]` under the claim, and the records under `A2`)
The bracket sits with the claim it settles, so a reader sees which questions decide it without leaving the sentence.
One question may be cited by more than one claim, which is how the ledger links a single probe answer to several claims.

### 4 · Stage Narrative

**What a narrative page owes**: a spine, one division per manuscript section, and a readiness tag on every beat.

```text
  🧵 STAGE NARRATIVE · order 3
  "How does the evidence compose into a section-mirrored,
   readiness-tagged story?"
  📄 S-Venue-2-narrative.md     🔨 draft · probe · revise · check
  ⚠️ family Venue, unit 2 ── the arc is Work's, the FAMILY is not

  📚 ## Content · one division PER SECTION OF THIS PAPER
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ### 1 · Spine (throughline)   problem ▸ move ▸ finding ▸ so-what
  ### 2 · Abstract              venue-section: abstract
  ### 3 · Introduction          venue-section: introduction
  ### 4 · Literature            venue-section: theory      ⟵ SPLIT
  ### 5 · Theory                venue-section: theory      ⟵ SPLIT
  ### 6 · Measurement           venue-section: methods     ⟵ SPLIT
  ### 7 · Empirical             venue-section: methods     ⟵ SPLIT
  ### 8 · Results               venue-section: results
  ### 9 · Discussion            venue-section: discussion
  ### 10 · Conclusion           venue-section: —           ⟵ ADDED
  ### 11 · Footer Ledger

  🏛 the LIST comes from the venue, not from the template
     playbook-utd-is/MISQ/MISQ-<section>/style.md ── 7 sections, measured
        ━━▶ S-Venue-0 Structural Blueprint ── transcribed, per section
        ━━▶ HERE ── one division per section THIS paper writes
        ━━▶ section-edit units_from ── one S-Main page per division
  📌 every division carries venue-section: and Adaptation:
     the style rules come FROM the venue section it points at
  🏷 every beat carries a READINESS tag: [LIT] · [READY] · …
  🪢 everything below the spine must serve that one line
```

🧵 Establishes the arc as a section-mirrored plan, and the tag that says which beat is actually ready.

#### 4.1 · The spine is one line, and everything below serves it
(the whole paper in one breath, as an arrow chain from the problem to the so-what)
It runs problem and stakes, then the move this paper introduces, then the core finding, then what it is and is not.
A beat anywhere below that does not serve the spine is a beat the arc does not need, which is the only pruning rule this stage has.

#### 4.2 · The section list comes from the venue, and this page adapts it
(the venue says how a paper of this journal reads; this page says how THIS paper is cut)
A venue playbook holds one folder per section with its own measured `style.md`, and MISQ has seven.
The venue stage transcribes those into `S-Venue-0`'s Structural Blueprint, and this page turns them into one division per section this paper actually writes.
A paper may SPLIT one venue section into two divisions, or ADD one the venue does not list, and each division records which venue section it obeys and why it diverged.
The style rules still come from the venue section a division points at, so splitting changes the cut and never the norms.

#### 4.3 · Each division is a story paragraph plus its key points
(draft-quality plain language first, then the numbered beats that carry it)
Each section division carries a short paragraph in plain language, then numbered key points to cover.
That is why this stage is the bridge to QB6 Main: the divisions are already the sections, so Main inherits an order rather than inventing one.

#### 4.4 · A readiness tag says which beat is real and which is still owed
(`[LIT]` needs a citation, `[READY]` is settled, and the legend defines the rest)
Every key point carries a tag, so the arc reports its own completeness rather than reading as finished prose.
That is what lets this concern's gate eventually test the arc: a section whose beats are all tagged owed is not ready, whatever the paragraph reads like.

## Glossary

- **Bank**: a store of evidence outside the paper, one for discovery and one for tasks, shared across papers.
- **Arc**: the reveal order of the claim ledger and the section list that follows from it, distinct from the wording, which is QB6 Main's.
- **Readiness tag**: the mark on a narrative beat that says whether it is settled or still owed, which is what lets an arc report its own completeness.
- **Work**: resource, claim, and probe coordination that turns the paper contract into bank-owned evidence, plus the order that evidence is revealed in.

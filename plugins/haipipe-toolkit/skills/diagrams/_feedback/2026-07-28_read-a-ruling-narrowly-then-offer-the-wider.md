JL, verbatim: "we will not use display01b, please list what displays we will have here."

The sentence has two readings: §5 drops the unit, or the paper drops it. They are very different. The
wider one would touch `S-Display-1b`, `S-Display-0`'s §4 block and `S-Main-4`'s Stage Contract.

What I did, and JL did not object: took the narrow reading because "here" meant §5, removed the unit
from §5's `requires:`, its Stage Contract and both its cards, changed nothing about the unit itself,
and wrote in both the reply and the page: "Read narrowly on purpose: JL said §5 will not use it, not
that the paper drops the unit."

The rule: take the narrow reading, SAY that you took it, and name the wider one so the user can
escalate in one word.

The corollary that matters more: a narrow ruling leaves residue, and the residue must be recorded
rather than silently absorbed. Here, the rehearsal cue that moved to §4 kept its §5 justification
word for word, "Once the five cohorts and their contrast are clear", while the five cohorts are
introduced at P5 of §5 and nowhere in §4.

## C1 · Overview · what the loop is, and how to read the rest

### C1.P1 · the loop stated once, before any single phase
- B1 · one page is worked by many hands over many days, and the same visible
  edit can be two different acts.
- B2 · a phase is defined by the one question it answers, not by the edit it
  performs.                                                              🎯 A1.1
- B3 · a page moves forward by satisfying a phase's exit, not by being worked
  on.                                                                    🎯 A1.1
- B4 · the Opening section says where this page sits. §1 says what the loop is.
  Neither repeats the other.

### C1.P2 · how to read the rest of it
- B1 · the whole loop on one picture: seven phases, who acts at each, which
  five stops need a person, and where a round restarts.
                                                🖼 Display4 · the-loop-at-a-glance
- B2 · the roadmap: fifteen divisions in four parts, one line each.
- B3 · the loop today, counted: how many phases, how many shipping contracts,
  how many person-reserved ticks, how many RUNs have executed and which phases
  they covered.                                                         🔢 value
- B4 · two reading paths. Run the loop: §1, §8, §14. Implement one phase: its
  division, then its contract file.

### C1.P3 · the four words this page spends
- B1 · workflow, phase, step and round are four different words, and one receipt
  spends all four.
- B2 · "step" would contradict the router's own name, which is RUN and not
  ADVANCE.
- B3 · the folder is already `page-workflows/`, so the workflow word is settled.
- B4 · a PAGE is the persistent source and rendered surface. An OPERATION is
  add, delete, move or rewrite.                                          🎯 A1.2
- B5 · the words are defined here because every division below spends them, and
  the old order defined them last.
- B6 · no other document in this family uses one of the four for another's
  meaning.                                                               🎯 A1.3

## C2 · OUTLINE ① · what the page will say, before it says it

### C2.P1 · what OUTLINE decides, and what it leaves alone
- B1 · OUTLINE asks what the page will say, division by division, and what each
  bullet still owes.                                                     🎯 A2.1
- B2 · it writes one file and nothing in the page itself. The plan and the page
  are two files.                                                         🎯 A2.1
- B3 · it marks a hole and stops. It raises no card and asks nothing.

### C2.P2 · the Aims are agreed here, not later
- B1 · the Aims are settled in this same file, and not at DRAFT. Shape and
  target are read once and approved by one tick.                         🎯 A2.2
- B2 · every bullet that owes something names its Aim by id, so no id can
  dangle.                                                                🎯 A2.2
- B3 · DRAFT transcribes those Aims onto the page rather than inventing them.

### C2.P3 · the tick that ends the phase
- B1 · it ends when a person ticks `approved:`. Nothing else ends it.
- B2 · the gate is the cheapest on the board: changing a section list costs one
  line before anything is written, and costs the whole section after.
- B3 · the file's shape, its marks and its versions live at QPw1-outline and
  haipipe-plugin-outline.

## C3 · DRAFT ② · turning each point into sentences

### C3.P1 · what DRAFT actually does, now that the Aims have left
- B1 · DRAFT's whole job is the conversion: one approved POINT becomes one or
  more SENTENCES on the page.                                            🎯 A3.1
- B2 · it no longer owns the Aims. They are settled at OUTLINE since 260819, and
  DRAFT transcribes them.
- B3 · it enters on an approved plan and names no division the plan did not
  name. A shape that turns out wrong is a v2 at OUTLINE, not a quiet edit here.
- B4 · an operation does not identify DRAFT. Adding a paragraph under an
  existing Aim is REVISE; the phase is named by its reason, never its diff.

### C3.P2 · one point becomes several sentences, and the hole stays visible
- B1 · the worked conversion, shown both ways round, so a reader can do it:
  the point, then the scaffold it becomes, with the address carried across.
                                                    🖼 Display5 · point-to-sentence
- B2 · a mark in the plan becomes a HOLE in the sentence, sitting exactly where
  the answer will sit, with the card id beside it.                       🎯 A3.2
- B3 · DRAFT may never invent the value, the citation, the reading or the
  figure. A sentence that already knows its answer was written after the fact.
- B4 · the backlink `realizes: C3.P1.B4` rides in a comment on the scaffold
  line, so REVISE may split or merge the sentence without losing the join.

### C3.P3 · how it is written, because a scaffold is still prose
- B1 · one idea per sentence, and a sentence past about thirty words is usually
  two.                                                                   🎯 A3.3
- B2 · the AI-tell catalogue applies here and not only at REVISE: a scaffold
  written in machine cadence is rewritten twice instead of once.         🎯 A3.3
- B3 · what a scaffold may NOT contain, as a short list a writer can check
  against: an invented number, a hedge standing in for a hole, a summary of a
  card, or a section the plan did not name.

## C5 · EVIDENCE ④ · landing what came back

### C5.P1 · the whole phase on one picture
- B1 · two stages, three lanes, what each lane returns, the three ticks, and
  where the prose starts.                           🖼 Display5 · evidence-logic
- B2 · EVIDENCE is MAKE then BIND, and both stages stay inside the plugin
  folders. Neither writes a sentence.                                    🎯 A5.4
- B3 · the boundary is what makes the phase auditable: if one phase both landed
  the answer and wrote the sentence, the only proof the answer came first would
  be its own report.

### C5.P2 · 📚 the citation lane
- B1 · what it lands: one entry in bibex/, and a key a sentence can cite.
- B2 · who makes it: a PERSON, verbatim. A machine may SUBSET or TRANSCRIBE
  bibtex and may never COMPOSE it.
- B3 · its tick is `verified`, on the entry itself, and it does not revert.
- B4 · a 📚 mark naming a sibling board page is the commonest error: that is a
  cross-reference, not a citation.

### C5.P3 · 🔢 the value lane
- B1 · what it lands: a number bound BY PATH to the QA file that answered it,
  with the pulled file in the card's proof/.
- B2 · who makes it: the bank, task or discovery, answering into its own QA
  file. PROBE already raised the card and asked.
- B3 · its tick is `read:`, and it REVERTS when `target:` changes or proof/ is
  re-pulled.
- B4 · one card holds several numbers, so the id goes one level deeper:
  `PP<NN>.v<n>`, allocated here in card.md's ## Values block.             🎯 A5.5
- B5 · which makes two failures visible: a number in the prose with no id, and
  a value nobody uses.

### C5.P4 · 🖼 the display lane
- B1 · what it lands: a DRAWN, previewable unit, not a folder of material.
- B2 · four of the walk's five steps are here: intake, render, pick, build.
  Only accept is CHECK's.
- B3 · they moved from REVISE on 260819, because this lane was the only one of
  three that returned nothing a page could use.
- B4 · whether it waits depends on the kind: a data unit freezes from a probe
  card's proof/, a concept unit freezes a listing of source files.
- B5 · its tick is `accepted: ✅`, it lives at CHECK, and it REVERTS when the
  intake changes.

### C5.P5 · what the three lanes have in common
- B1 · they run at the same time. None waits for another to finish.      🎯 A5.1
- B2 · each returns something a page can use: a key, a bound number, a drawn
  figure.                                                                🎯 A5.3
- B3 · three ticks live here, more than in any other phase, and two of the three
  revert because acceptance binds to the inputs it was accepted with.    🎯 A5.2
- B4 · a tick belongs to stage BIND, because it is what turns a made thing into
  one a sentence may quote.
- B5 · how much of EVIDENCE has ever run on this board.                  🔢 value

## Notes for DRAFT · the mechanics, kept out of the plan

**The renumber costs 52 `§N` anchors and 16 Aim ids.** Zero deletions. The Aim
remap is a bijection, so it is mechanical, but it must land in ONE pass or the
🧭 tab mis-anchors every remapped row.

```text
  A11 ──▶ A1     A1 ──▶ A3     A2 ──▶ A4     A3 ──▶ A6     A4 ──▶ A8
  A5  ──▶ A9     A6 ──▶ A10    A7 ──▶ A11    A8 ──▶ A12    A9 ──▶ A13
  A10 ──▶ A14    A12 ──▶ A15
  new: A2 (OUTLINE) · A5 (EVIDENCE) · A7 (COMPILE) · A1.1 (overview)
```

Sub-paragraph numbers in this plan are the PROPOSED ones. Today's §2.4 is §4.4
here; today's §9.1 is §13.1. Old §10's three rules lose their numbers, because
the fold makes them §1.2's.

**This is DRAFT-shaped work, not REVISE-shaped.** Four new divisions create new
Aims, and creating an Aim is DRAFT's authority. The purpose does not move; the
Aim set does.

## Aims · what must become true, and how you would know

Every Aim is one target plus its test. `Done when:` IS the test: the sentence
that says how a reader could check the Aim without asking anyone.

The Aims live in this file rather than on the page (JL 260819: "Aims should be
move together with outline"). Before that, the plan wrote an id and the page
held the target, so a plan that renumbered its divisions pointed `A5.1` at the
page's OLD A5.1. Nine of sixteen ids did exactly that, and the tab showed a
confidently wrong aim.

- A1.1 · The loop is stated once, whole, before any single phase is argued.
  **Done when:** a reader can stop after §1 and say what the seven phases are,
  which one they are in, and where to read next.
- A1.2 · No document in this family uses one of the four words for another's
  meaning.
  **Done when:** a sweep finds no receipt field, contract sentence, or page
  division calling a phase a step or a step a phase.
- A1.3 · The four are readable from one figure without opening the receipt.
  **Done when:** each word carries what it answers, its count in a real run, and
  whether it repeats.
- A2.1 · OUTLINE has a division of its own, stating its authority, its
  deliverable and the tick that ends it.
  **Done when:** the body opens on phase ① rather than phase ②, and a reader
  reaches DRAFT already knowing what an approved plan is.
- A2.2 · The Aims are settled during OUTLINE, in the plan file, and every
  owing bullet links to one by id.
  **Done when:** no bullet names an Aim the plan does not declare, and DRAFT
  transcribes the Aims rather than inventing them.
- A3.1 · DRAFT is defined by the point-to-sentence conversion, not by first
  creation and not by owning Aims.
  **Done when:** a reader can identify DRAFT in both an empty Page and a mature
  Page that reopens its promise, and can say what DRAFT kept when the Aims left.
- A3.2 · Every mark in the plan becomes a visible hole in the sentence that will
  use it, with its card id beside it.
  **Done when:** no landed answer can be written into prose without passing
  through a hole, and no hole exists that no card serves.
- A3.3 · A scaffold reads like a person wrote it, before REVISE touches it.
  **Done when:** `score.py` on a fresh DRAFT output flags no more sentences than
  it flags on the same page's already-revised prose.
- A4.1 · PROBE has an explicit trigger, write surface, return record, and exit.
  **Done when:** a reader can route a consequential unknown without letting
  PROBE author target prose.
- A4.2 · The target Page, Probe surface, sibling handoff, and forbidden
  cross-Page write are unambiguous.
  **Done when:** a family can file a QA-probe by evidence route without silently
  transferring Q-consumer ownership or authoring a sibling Page in the same run.
- A4.3 · One Probe reference can show the full evidence chain without becoming a
  second authored answer.
  **Done when:** the reference drives render projection, bounded phase context,
  dependency versioning, visible failure, and CHECK.
- A5.1 · EVIDENCE is readable as parallel lanes, and the four marks a plan can
  carry are named against the three lanes that exist.
  **Done when:** a reader can say why proof is the fourth kind and has no lane,
  without opening another contract.
- A5.2 · The three ticks EVIDENCE carries are stated with which two revert.
  **Done when:** a reader can name the input whose change reverts `read:` and
  the one that reverts `accepted:`.
- A5.3 · What each lane returns is stated side by side.
  **Done when:** the three returns are one block, not three paragraphs in three
  contracts.
- A5.4 · EVIDENCE is readable as two stages, MAKE then BIND, with the boundary
  to prose visible.
  **Done when:** a reader can say which stage a person's tick belongs to, and
  why neither stage may write a sentence.
- A5.5 · A value is addressable on its own, not only through the card that holds
  it.
  **Done when:** every number in this page's prose carries a `PP<NN>.v<n>`, and
  no card holds a value no sentence uses.
- A6.1 · REVISE is separated from DRAFT by whether purpose and Aims remain
  fixed.
  **Done when:** the same add, delete, move, or rewrite operation can be
  classified from its reason.
- A6.2 · COMPILE's fold into REVISE is stated as a measured decision with its
  open ruling named, not as an inherited silence.
  **Done when:** the division scores COMPILE against §10's four split tests and
  points at QPw5-revise for the ruling.
- A6.2 · CHECK judges one version and routes it to close, REVISE, PROBE, or a
  new DRAFT.
  **Done when:** every finding names the authority that owns the next change.
- A8.1 · Repetition, optional phases, and REVISE to DRAFT are represented
  without contradiction.
  **Done when:** `DRAFT, DRAFT, PROBE, REVISE, DRAFT` has an unambiguous round
  interpretation.
- A9.1 · Common Page edits are examples rather than phase definitions.
  **Done when:** adding, deleting, moving, and rewriting each have both a DRAFT

- A10.1 · Each phase remains a Content division until it becomes an

  **Done when:** the Page names the four split tests and distinguishes phase

- A11.1 · The skill tree keeps persistent Page variation separate from current

  **Done when:** Page Types live under `page-types/`, Page Phases live under
  `page-workflows/`, and the base routes both without introducing an Entry phase

- A12.1 · One automatic router composes phase producers, version snapshots,

  **Done when:** `display/QPw00-Display3-who-does-what/intake/manifest.yaml`

- A14.2 · The TIME column stops being an estimate.
  **Done when:** `_runs/page/` holds at least one receipt for each of OUTLINE,

- P1 · The base Page contract either adopts this lifecycle vocabulary or

  **Done when:** JL chooses the base-adoption option and the affected contracts

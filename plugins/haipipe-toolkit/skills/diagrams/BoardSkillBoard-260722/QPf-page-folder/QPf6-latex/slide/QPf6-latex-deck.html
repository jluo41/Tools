The page compiled, in its own folder

A page's .md is the source; its .tex and .pdf are DERIVED. The paper family's md2tex writes them by path — never copied — and a rebuild may overwrite them. This talk settles the plugin's contract and names what the 260815 build left open.

Open with the question the page opens with: where does a page's LaTeX projection live, and who is allowed to write it? The answer is the derived-plugin law — the page's own latex folder, regenerable at one click. Promise both halves: what shipped, and what's still owed.

§1 · The contract

Two files, both derived, both regenerable

⟨page⟩/latex/
  ⟨stem⟩.tex   ⚙️ md2tex's section · "do not hand-edit" header
  ⟨stem⟩.pdf   ⚙️ the compiled look · what the tab frames

flat page fallback: ⟨board⟩/latex/⟨stem⟩.*
                    the same fork deck.py takes

A hand-edited file is overwritten on the next build — that is the derived-plugin rule, not a defect.

Stress that overwrite-on-rebuild is the contract working as intended. If you hand-edit the tex, the next click erases you — and that's the rule from QPf1 that every derived plugin shares, not a bug in this one.

§ Diagram · one source, four hops

Markdown to framed PDF, nothing typed by hand

📄 ⟨page⟩/⟨stem⟩.md              the SOURCE · haipipe-page owns it
      │  POST /_board/latex      live/export.py
      ▼

Walk the four hops top to bottom. The twist worth saying aloud: md2tex's own --compile is bound to one paper's hand-written master, so the board wraps the generated section in a generic article master and compiles that instead — then deletes the master residue after every run.

§1 · Where the citations come from

The page's own bib first, then the paper's, then none

📚 Page bib wins

bibex/⟨stem⟩.bib is preferred when it holds an entry — JL 260815: the page's cites should compile.

🚶 Walk-up discovery

No page bib? --paper-root is found by walking up toward --root for a 0-*.bib.

🕊 Cite-less, not refused

Outside any paper, \citep prints as [key] and the export still compiles.

With either bib the master gains natbib, plainnat, and a bibtex pass — QPf8's PDF proved it: [Luo et al., 2026] inline plus a real References page.

This is a graceful-degradation ladder: the workbench's own store first, the surrounding paper second, and a legible fallback third. The design choice to defend is the last rung — the export never refuses, it just prints the key.

§2 · What the writer inherits, and what it drops

The grammar mostly coincides; the misses are recorded

### 1 · title      ──▶  \section        maps cleanly
#### block         ──▶  paragraph break
\citep{} \ref{}    ──▶  kept verbatim   already LaTeX
> lanes            ──▶  DROPPED         nowhere to go in TeX
refuse-to-regress  ──▶  inherited       lost citations = refusal

The gap: xelatex drops glyphs its fonts lack, so an emoji-heavy ascii figure reads thinner in PDF than on stage — A2.1 measures that loss instead of guessing it.

Note that lanes become comments in Word but simply vanish here — same writer, different ceilings per projection. And be honest that the proof run, QPf4b's four-page PDF, was prose-heavy and flattered the mapping; the figure-dense stress test hasn't run yet.

§3 · The surface

The 📜 tab: the Slides sandwich, compiler as filling

82-plugin-exports.js ─POST─▶ /_board/latex ─runs─▶ md2tex + xelatex
tab.url()    names latex/⟨stem⟩.pdf · HEAD hit ▶ frame it
tab.write()  builds on miss · lit-click        ▶ REBUILD
failure      ▶ a view page: the .tex + log tail · never blank

A3.1 met 260815: QPf4b's four-page PDF framed live in a real browser, opened from the Plugin menu, closed by its own ✕ — driven, not assumed.

The one line to land here is the failure contract: a compile error never leaves a blank frame — you get the tex and the log tail as a page instead of silence. And the tab was proven by actually driving a browser through it, which is why A3 is checked.

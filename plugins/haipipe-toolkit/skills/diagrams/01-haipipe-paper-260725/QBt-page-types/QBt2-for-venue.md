## Opening

The Journal of Imaginary Systems does not exist. It was invented so this page could be a real venue page with no chance that a reader mistakes its rules for a real desk's rules. A venue page records a desk you cannot argue with: what it buys, what a paper is built out of, and what the portal demands on the day you upload. So what does this invented desk demand, and what does it refuse?

🚫 **Everything on this page is fabricated.** The desk, its word cap, its reference style, its odds, its fees and its URLs are all invented. Its host is `jis.example.invalid`, and `.invalid` is a name RFC 2606 reserves so that it can never resolve to anything real. Cite none of it and follow none of it. A wrong word cap that reads as real could cost somebody a real submission, which is why the mark sits in the title, here, and inside every figure on the page that carries a number.

**The `page-type: venue` line above is not in the contract's table**: step ③ of the base's type resolution defines exactly four values, `display`, `slide`, `design` and `section`, and `venue` is not one of them. A venue page is meant to resolve at step ①, by a `QBv<n>-` filename this page does not wear, so the only key that matches this file is `QBt2-` at step ⑤ and the resolver reads it as a Q decision page. The key is inert as well: `page-type` appears in no file under `haipipe-board/src/` or `haipipe-board/cli/`, so no code reads it on any page, which is what `QBt3` and `QBt9` record for their own keys. Whether the table grows or both keys go is one `### Decision Now` row on `QBt1-for-stage`, which carries the twin of this line with `stage`; this page states the fact and makes no call.

**How to read this page**: everything on it is a REFERENCE except one figure. The arcs, the bands, the moves and the refusals describe what the pack prescribes for a JIS paper, and a drafter may depart from any of them on purpose. `Submission-Rules` is different: those are the desk's own published rules, and at a real desk a manuscript that breaks one is returned unreviewed. Every length on this page says which of the two it is.

**Whose number is whose, and a third label this desk needs**: `QBv1-misq.md`, the contract's reference implementation on the paper board, splits every length into a DESK RULE, which binds, and a PACK OBSERVATION, which is the pack's measurement of published papers. This pack has measured nothing, because no JIS paper exists. So its bands are marked PACK PRESCRIPTION, a target somebody wrote down, which is weaker than an observation and much weaker than a rule. The contract already met this at `QBv15-grant`, whose pack holds zero funded proposals, and ruled that a page in that position says so rather than borrowing the stronger word.

**Where its things are**: a page's companion folder is `<type-plural>/<page name>/`, so this page's pack is `venue/QBt2-for-venue/`. That is the group's one naming rule, and it collides with how real packs are named: a real pack is named after the OUTLET, as `playbook-utd-is/MISQ/` is, and it lives in a different repository. Both facts are true, the group rule is followed here, and the collision is written down in §2 rather than smoothed over.

**Why this page declares no `output:`**: because the folder named after it is an INPUT it does not own. The three specimens in this group each have a companion, and each one is owned by somebody different. `QBt3`'s `_fixture-qbt/displays/QBt3-for-display/` is an OUTPUT the page builds. `QBt5`'s `QA-probe/QBt5-for-value/` is a RECORD its probes write. This page's `venue/QBt2-for-venue/` is a pack: read on every pass, written on none, and at a real board it is a pinned submodule of somebody else's repository. A venue page records a desk; it manufactures nothing.

**What every section division here is missing, and why**: no `n.4 · The language, in the papers' own words`.

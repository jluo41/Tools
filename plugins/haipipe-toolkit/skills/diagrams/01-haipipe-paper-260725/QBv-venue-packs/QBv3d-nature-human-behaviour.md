## Opening

Two of this desk's three rejections are about who was studied and one is about whether anything was explained. Together they make an unusual demand: prove the behavior generalizes, and say why it happens. So what does that rule out?

**Where this page sits**: it is one outlet under `QBv3`, which owns what the Nature portfolio's five outlets share.
This page owns only what is true of `playbook-nature-portfolio/nature-human-behaviour/`.

**Why this outlet is relevant to this repo at all**: physician-trait work is behavioral evidence at scale, and this is the portfolio's behavioral desk.
Its cross-cultural bar is the reason it has never been the obvious target, and that reason has not been written down anywhere until now.

**What is unscored**: sample composition. Nothing in the lifecycle records which population a study drew from, so the desk's central rejection cannot be checked.

## Content

### 1 · The rejection is about the population

**WEIRD-only sold as universal**: not the sample size, and not the method.

```text
  👥 what the desk is refusing
     a sample drawn from Western, educated, industrialized,
     rich, democratic populations, presented as a claim
     about humans

  💡 note the phrase: "SOLD as universal"
     ── the sample is not the defect, the CLAIM about it is
     ── so this rejection IS fixable at revision, unlike
        Nature Medicine's validation bar (QBv3b)

  ⚠️ but only if the paper knows what its sample was
     ── and nothing in this repo's lifecycle records that
```

👥 Establishes this as a claim-scope rejection rather than a design rejection, which makes it the rare Nature bar a rewrite can clear.

#### 1.1 · A US-claims-data study is a bounded population, not a universal one
(which is the shape this repo's own work has)
CMS Medicare data describes US physicians under one payment system.
Framed as a claim about that population it is honest; framed as a claim about physician behavior it is the exact rejection this desk names.

### 2 · Mechanism is the second, separate demand

**Descriptive at scale still fails**: large-N does not substitute for why.

```text
  🧠 the third rejection stands alone
     "descriptive social-media analysis with no mechanism"
     ── the N can be enormous and the paper still fails

  🔗 the same demand, phrased per field across this tree
     🏛 MISQ    a specified mechanism, not an association
     🔬 ISR     a NAMED mechanism per hypothesis
     📈 MS-IS   an economic mechanism or equilibrium
     🧠 NHB     a BEHAVIORAL mechanism

  💡 so a paper carrying a mechanism can often be re-argued
     across these four desks; one carrying only an
     association can be argued into none of them
```

🧠 Establishes the mechanism demand as shared across four desks in two packs, which makes it the single most portable property a paper can have.

## Law

This desk's central rejection is a claim-scope error rather than a design error: the sample is not the defect, selling it as universal is, so a paper that states its population honestly can clear a bar that the same data sold wider cannot.
A mechanism demand is shared by four desks across two packs, which makes carrying one the most portable property a paper has.

## Glossary

- **WEIRD**: Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic; the sample composition this desk refuses to see presented as a universal claim.
- **Claim-scope error**: overstating the population a finding covers, fixable at revision, as against a design error, which is not.

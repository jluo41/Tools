## Opening

PNAS runs roughly six pages, about fifty thousand characters. What does a venue that tight ask for first?

The pack's own taste file makes that gate the test. If you cannot write the significance statement, the paper is not ready, and no amount of rewriting the Introduction fixes it.

**Where this page sits**: it is one pack under `QBv0`, which owns what any pack owes.
This page owns only what is true of `playbook-pnas` and its single outlet.

**Why this pack sits at a different level from its siblings**: `taste.md` and `examples/` live at FAMILY level here, not at outlet level, because the pack has exactly one outlet.
`QBv0` A3.1 owns that split; this page is one of the three cases that forced it.

**What is structurally unusual**: `pnas/pnas-significance/` is a section folder for a component no other outlet in the tree has.
It is not an abstract and not an introduction; it is a separate, non-technical statement of why the finding matters, and it is the gate the editor reads first.

## Writing Style

How this page must be written. Read it before editing, and edit to it.

**Inherited from `QB4`**: the page grammar, the section order, and the sentence rules come from `QB4-overall.md` and are not restated here.

**Never transcribe the length numbers**: they live in `playbook-pnas/` and are cited, never copied.

**Treat the significance statement as a gate, not a section**: the pack's taste file uses it as a readiness test, and calling it a section makes it sound like something written last.

✅ `if you cannot write it, the paper is not ready`  ❌ `remember to add a significance statement`

**Say the outlet folder is `pnas/` inside `playbook-pnas/`**: the doubled name is real and confuses every path written from memory.

### 1 · The significance statement is the gate

**Read first, written first**: the pack's taste file uses it as the readiness test, not as a deliverable.

```text

      if you can't write it, the paper isn't ready)."
                        ── playbook-pnas/taste.md

  ── a desk-reject signal phrased as a self-test
  ── which makes it usable BEFORE the paper exists,
     unlike every other desk-reject signal in the tree
```

🚪 Establishes the statement as an upstream gate, which is the only reason this outlet can be evaluated from a seed.

#### 1.1 · It is the one venue test in the tree a seed can take
(so PNAS fit is decidable at QB1 Opening, with no evidence and no draft)
Every other outlet's desk-reject signals need a study design or a result to score against.
This one needs a sentence, which means the venue stage can run it against a bare topic on the `--no-pin` path.

### 2 · The ceiling changes what the paper may contain

**Six pages is a design constraint, not an editing pass**: it decides how many claims survive.

```text
  📏 ~6 pages · ~50,000 characters
     ❌ "long, discursive papers that could be cut by half"

  💥 the cost lands on QB4 Value and QB5 Display:
     a claim ledger sized for MISQ does not fit
     a display set sized for Nature Medicine does not fit

  ── the pack's ->Claims and ->Display maps exist for
     exactly this reason
```

📏 Establishes the length ceiling as a claims-and-displays decision, which is why the pack maps to those two stages rather than to prose alone.

#### 2.1 · A retarget into PNAS is a subtraction
(and subtraction is the retarget direction no other pack in this tree describes)
UTD-IS adds a theory section, npj Digital Medicine adds a related-work section.
PNAS removes: fewer claims, fewer floats, and a Methods that moves toward the supplement.

## Law

The PNAS significance statement is a readiness gate before it is a section: a paper that cannot state it in non-technical prose is not ready, whatever its results.
A retarget into PNAS subtracts claims and floats rather than adding sections, so the cost lands on the claim ledger and the display set, not on the prose.

## Glossary

- **Significance Statement**: PNAS's required non-technical statement of why a finding matters, read before the abstract and used by this pack as a readiness test.
- **Family-level placement**: keeping `taste.md` and `examples/` in the pack folder rather than the outlet folder, the convention used by the three single-outlet and non-journal packs.

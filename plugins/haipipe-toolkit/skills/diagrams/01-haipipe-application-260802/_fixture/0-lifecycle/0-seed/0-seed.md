0-seed: 01_sms_young_male (why this might work, venue-free)
==========================================================

Date: 2026-07-18
Status: DRAFT
The earliest-stage contract: keeps the intervention possibility alive before evidence is mature.


Opportunity
-----------

The SMS-nudge evidence base is built almost entirely on female-majority samples, and the few trials that estimate heterogeneous effects find young men benefit LEAST from standard message framing.
No prior work designs SMS message framing specifically for young male patients (18-35); the gap is comprehensive across adherence, prevention, and health promotion.
DrFirst's SMSR2Full experiment (751K invitations across 13 behavioral framing arms) is a rare asset that can identify which framing arm actually moves this documented low-responder segment.
Target behavior: lift young-male engagement with medication/refill SMS along the open -> click -> refill funnel.


Expected impact
---------------

Generic adherence texts move refill roughly 2pp and are frequently non-significant (Ho et al., JAMA 2025); ownership-style framing moved flu vaccination +3.3pp (Buttenheim et al., JAMA Netw Open 2022).
By selecting the best-performing framing arm for the young-male segment (HTE-driven) rather than sending a generic message, the directional estimate is a 2-5pp lift in young-male SMS engagement over the status-quo/generic arm.


Audience
--------

Young male patients (roughly 18-35) in the SMSR2Full cohort who receive medication and refill SMS.
A specific subset, not "all patients": this segment is chosen because the outside literature identifies it as the systematically under-served, low-responding group for behavioral text messaging.


Channel hunch
-------------

SMS.
This is a hunch for context, not a commitment; the venue is pinned after the 1a-1d evidence ladder via /haipipe-application venue, and the SMSR2Full experiment is itself an SMS field study, so SMS is the natural first guess.


Mechanism hypothesis
--------------------

Young men disengage from clinical or paternalistic SMS but respond to direct, no-nonsense, autonomy-supportive framing with light humor, so matching the framing ARM to these preferences (rather than matching advisor gender, which produced a null result in Royer et al. 2026) should lift engagement where the generic message underperforms.

----------

By theory and insight
=====================

One design method card. Guide › Method shows it among its method cards; its
papers are the rows of `../design-papers.md` whose `group` is `by theory and
insight`. A claim names its source in brackets; "(ours)" marks the workbench's own
judgment.

family: Internal Insights: abduction-1 on our own induction; the how comes from our own
  data: signed insights, past designs, or readers who try the drafts
also: External Insights (it reads a named theory as well as our signed insights)
reasoning: abduction-1 on two hows: requirements + a signed insight (what worked here) +
  a theory (why it worked) → a design both support; a disagreement goes to the next Exp
move: Make the design follow a signed insight that a named theory explains, and predict
  the one variable it moves.
taxonomy: Evidence and theory-based, both halves: a theory or theoretical framework
  combined with data from multiple sources [O'Cathain 2019 taxonomy].
comes from: Michie 2011, Behaviour Change Wheel; Bartholomew 1998, Intervention Mapping;
  Sackett 1996, evidence-based practice
reads: design requirements · internal insights: signed rows · external insights: named
  theories
returns: the design, the insight row and the theory each part rests on, and where they
  disagree
test now: T1 the cited row and the technique both present · T2 critique of the fit
test in use: T4 against the control


What the literature says
------------------------

rationale: The problem can be understood through the people and setting it serves, or
  through theory and published research; some approaches use both, and weigh them
  differently [O'Cathain 2019 taxonomy]. An intervention should be linked to an analysis
  of the behaviour it targets [Michie 2011].
context: Behaviour change interventions [Michie 2011]; health education programs
  [Bartholomew 1998]; clinical practice [Sackett 1996].
steps: Combine a theory or theoretical framework with data from multiple sources
  (interviews, focus groups, questionnaires, observation, documents, the literature) and
  with stakeholders, then prioritise the issues to change [O'Cathain 2019 taxonomy].
  Map the behaviour's determinants to behaviour change techniques [O'Cathain 2019
  taxonomy].
strengths: Each source covers the other's gap: theory use alone was not consistently
  linked to effect [Prestwich 2013], and an insight from past designs may not carry to a
  new wording (ours). A design both support is the strongest bet the inputs allow (ours).
limitations: No study tests it against using one source alone (ours). When the theory
  and the insight disagree, which should decide is a judgment, not a rule (ours).


Applied to AI
-------------

agent: The agent reads the design requirements, the signed DO and DO NOT rows of the
  InsightBoard's Wisdom pages, and the board's named message theories.
steps: 1. Pick the signed row the design will follow. 2. Name the theory that explains
  why that row worked, and the one variable it moves. 3. Write the design so both are
  present. 4. Where the theory predicts something the row does not support, follow the
  row and name the gap as a question for the next Exp.
returns: The design, its because row, its theory and variable, its prediction, and any
  disagreement named.
verify: Verify checks that the cited row says what the design claims and that the
  technique is present (T1); a reviewer agent critiques whether the theory really
  explains the row (T2); the Exp tests the prediction (T4).
risk: A theory picked after the row only decorates it, so name the theory's prediction
  before writing (ours). Two sources that say the same thing make a longer reason, not a
  stronger one (ours).
evidence on AI: No study tests an AI designing from both kinds of insight yet (ours).
skill: haipipe-design-by-theory-and-insight (proposed)

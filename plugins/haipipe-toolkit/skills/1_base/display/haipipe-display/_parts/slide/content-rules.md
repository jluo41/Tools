# Content rules: what a slide says

1. **One idea per slide.** A second idea is a second slide.
2. **The headline tells the story.** "Usage doubled in six months" beats "Key statistics"; an open idea may end in "?". Never a contrast headline ("A Studio is the talk, not the folder?"): it reads as AI (JL 261029); ask plainly or name the options.
3. **Final text only.** No "TBD", no "[insert]"; every word on the slide is written out in the content.
4. **Self-contained.** No "like slide 2"; everything a slide needs is in its own content.
5. **Real numbers, with their source.** A number comes from a verified source, never from the image model.
6. **No filler.** No "Thank you" page, no "Questions?", no clichés ("dive into", "explore", "journey").
7. **One deck, one look.** The style, icon kind, colours and layout grid stay the same across a deck.
8. **Short text.** A headline of at most 10 words; labels of one to four words; small text is where pictures misspell.

A slide's content block names, in order: the headline; each group of text with what is drawn beside it; the layout
(what is where); the focal point. Example:

```text
Headline (top-left, large): "A Block is a promise to one reader?"
Three option cards across the middle, joined by a wavy line:
 Card A "A topic": a folder with a tag; caption "one subject in a folder"
 Card B "A stage": three boxes raw → records → cases; caption "one step of a pipeline"
 Card C "A promise", circled in orange with a small tag "leaning": a person receiving a parcel of question marks
Bottom-right sticky note with a big question mark: "Still open: does a data stage have a reader?"
Focal point: card C.
```

**A flow or life-cycle slide names its actors and labels every arrow** (JL 261029, from the labeling use-case slides): boxes for who acts (Human, Agent, the work) with one line under each, two arrows between each pair saying what moves each way, the outputs a round makes below, and the loop back drawn and labelled. Never a row of stage stones with one word each.

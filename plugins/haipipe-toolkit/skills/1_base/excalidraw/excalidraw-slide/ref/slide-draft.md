# A slide draft (s61-s69)

A studio topic that proposes a deck before it is built (JL 261007: "one type is about propose the
slide draft"). The person reads the argument and every slide on one canvas, marks it, and only then
is the deck written. Moved here from haipipe-studio's `ref/topic-contract.md` (261007), words kept;
the topic folder, its number band, its face and its sessions stay haipipe-studio's.

## The ask, before the draft

Learned on j51 (261012; the slide job to copy, SKILL.md): before any slide, a topic of its own says what
they want, so every slide answers something that was asked. On j51 it is `s01-what-they-want`, read from a
36-minute meeting; its frames are the ask at a glance, your time, what hurts and the slide that answers it,
later (not now), and open.

| what the ask holds | on j51 |
|---|---|
| who the deck is for, how long, when it is shown | leadership and product · five minutes · 2026-10-08, after 11:00 |
| what is due when | 8 dates: send the slides 10-07 evening, the demo 10-08, the onsite next week … |
| what hurts, and the slide that answers it | 6 pains, each with its slide: "lessons flow past like water" → slide 12 |
| later, not now | 4 things named for after the demo, and whether the deck says them |
| open | who is in the room; a .pptx or a PDF; fourteen slides for five minutes |

1. **Where it was said**: every line carries its place in the source (a meeting's timestamp, an email's
   date); people by role only.
2. **Each slide answers it**: a slide's `source` names the line of the ask it answers; a pain that no slide
   answers is an open question, in red.
3. **An ordinary topic**: made with `new_topic.py topic <job> --slug what-they-want --band concept`, drawn
   by excalidraw-studio's principles; this skill only asks that it exists before `slides.py`.

## The folder

```text
<job>/studio/
├── s01-what-they-want/          the ask (above)
└── s6N-<deck>/
    ├── s6N-<deck>.md            face: audience, length, when it is shown · Decided · Open
    ├── slides.py                the deck's content, one entry per slide: the ONE source
    ├── build_s6N_<deck>.py      draws the drawing from slides.py
    ├── s6N-<deck>.excalidraw    frames left to right: 1 · logic flow · 2 · the slides · 3 · on disk, open
    ├── capture_screens.py       retakes screens/ from a running host (when a slide shows a screen)
    ├── screens/ · figures/      the pictures a `screen` or `figure` visual names by `file`
    ├── build_deck.py            the deck writer: reads the same slides.py
    └── delivery/                GENERATED by build_deck.py, never edited
        └── <deck>.pptx · .pdf
```

1. **One source**: `slides.py` holds each slide's title, one-sentence headline, ONE visual (its kind and
   data), caption, source, the reason the next slide follows, and speaker notes. The drawing and the
   deck both read it, so they cannot drift; the deck is generated, never edited.
2. **The deck lives with its draft** (JL 261007: "we can have the delivery in the s61 as well"): in the
   topic's own `delivery/`, so the draft, its source and the deck sit in one folder. The level's
   `delivery/` names it with a pointer, never a copy.
3. **Logic flow**: one box per slide, top to bottom, its title and headline; the words on each arrow
   say why the next slide follows; the acts (why · how · now …) bracket the boxes at the left.
4. **The slides**: every slide at half size (800 × 450 of 1600 × 900), two columns, in one skeleton:
   title and rule, headline, the one visual, caption, slide number.
5. **Frames left to right**: logic flow · the slides · on disk with open under it.
6. **The colour exception**: around the slides, the studio look (black; red = an open question; green =
   a change). Inside a slide, one accent marks the slide's key thing (`ACCENT`, `ACCENT_FILL`; the
   title rule too) and `MUTED` its caption, as the deck will. Check with `slide_kit.off_palette(els)`.
   The studio writer leaves a slide draft's colours as drawn (haipipe-studio `canvas.is_slide_draft`).
7. **Change notes under their slide** (JL 261008, on one long green list under the title: "why this is
   not under each slide?"): each green `✎ <YYMMDD> <what changed>` sits where it changed. A builder
   keeps `CHANGES` as `(where, date, what)`: `where` is a slide's `n` (drawn in green under that slide;
   the row of slides grows to fit), `"disk"` (under the folder tree in frame
   3) or `"all"` (under the title, for a change to the whole draft). A note about one slide is not
   repeated in the title, an exception to haipipe-studio's change-note rule. A note about something
   that no longer exists (a removed slide, an old table) is dropped with it.
8. **Then the deck**: once the drawing is agreed, `build_deck.py` builds from `slides.py` to the
   deck quality bar (the deck's template layouts, a native table or one rendered figure per slide).

## slides.py

One Python module, data only. Module fields:

| field | what |
|---|---|
| `AUDIENCE` | who the deck is for |
| `LENGTH` | how long it runs |
| `SHOWN` | when and where it is shown |
| `GROUPS` | the acts, in order: `(key, name, what it does)`, e.g. why · how · now |
| `SLIDES` | the slides, in order (below) |
| `OPEN` | the deck-wide open questions (length, template), drawn in red in frame 3; a question about one slide goes in that slide's `ask` |

Each slide, a dict:

| field | what |
|---|---|
| `n` | its number, two digits, in order: `"01"`, `"02"` … |
| `slug` | lower-case kebab, unique in the deck |
| `group` | the key of its act in `GROUPS` |
| `title` | the slide's title |
| `headline` | one sentence: what the audience should take away |
| `visual` | ONE visual: `{"kind": <kind>, … its data …, "hl": <what the accent marks>}` |
| `caption` | one line under the visual, in `MUTED` |
| `source` | where its content comes from |
| `next` | why the next slide follows (the words on the logic-flow arrow); empty on the last |
| `notes` | speaker notes |
| `ask` | optional: our open questions about this slide, a list of strings, drawn in red with the slide on the canvas (JL 261008: "you can use the red color to ask question"); whether a deck shows them too is that deck's choice |

A visual's `kind` names a renderer, the same in the drawing and in the deck. Kinds are the deck's own; a
new kind needs a renderer in both builders. j51's builders render eleven (its deck uses eight): cover · timeline (with `bullets` under its
boxes) · cycle · flow · table · tree · cards · converge (the arms into one new experiment) · pains (a pain
today → what the loop gives) · screen · figure. `hl` names the one thing the accent marks.

## Screens and figures

A `screen` (a workbench, an app) or a `figure` (a design, a concept figure) is a picture file beside
`slides.py`, named by the visual's `file` (`{"kind": "screen", "file": "screens/insight-workbench.png"}`).

1. **A screen is retaken, never pasted**: a script in the topic takes it from a running host (j51's
   `capture_screens.py`, headless Chrome, 1600 x 900), so a changed screen is one rerun.
2. **A figure keeps its own shape**: drawn whole, never cropped to fill the slide.
3. **Checked**: `slide_kit.py check` fails a visual whose `file` is not on disk and warns on a screen or
   figure that names none.

```bash
python <excalidraw-slide>/scripts/slide_kit.py check studio/s6N-<deck>/slides.py
```

The check is mechanical: the module fields are there, slides are numbered in order, slugs are kebab
and unique, each group is in `GROUPS`, each visual names a kind and its `file` is on disk, each `next`
is filled but the last.
Whether the argument holds is the person's read of the drawing.

## The deck writer

`build_deck.py` lives in the topic for now: it reads `SLIDES`, lays out each slide in the one
skeleton (title · headline · visual · caption · notes), renders each visual by its kind, and writes
`delivery/<deck>.pptx` and `.pdf`. Start a new one from j51's (python-pptx: native boxes, arrows and
tables; a picture for a screen or figure; 16:9, white). Once its generic part (read, skeleton, write, one
renderer per kind) is separate from a deck's own renderers, it moves into this skill's `scripts/`.

What a deck writer must do (learned on the first deck, 261008):

1. **PDF export through PowerPoint**: export a temporary copy of the .pptx, never the file itself.
   Opening the file by path returns the person's window if it is already open (possibly an older
   version), and closing it after export closes their window.

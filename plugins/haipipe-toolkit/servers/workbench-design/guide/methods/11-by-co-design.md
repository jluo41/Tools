By co-design
============

One design method card. Guide › Method shows it among its method cards; its
papers are the rows of `../../related/papers.md` whose `group` is `by co-design`. A
claim names its source in brackets; "(ours)" marks the workbench's own judgment.

family: Internal Insights: abduction-1 on our own induction; the how comes from our own
  data: signed insights, past designs, or readers who try the drafts
reasoning: induction from people's choices: they pick among the AI's options, and their
  reasons give the how
move: Design with the people it is for: they help decide what the design should do, and
  choose among the options.
status: future: a method to add later; no task is designed this way yet
taxonomy: Partnership: the people an intervention aims to help take part in its
  decisions throughout development, with at least an equal say [O'Cathain 2019
  taxonomy].
comes from: O'Cathain 2019 taxonomy, partnership: co-creation, co-production, co-design;
  Voorberg 2015, co-creation with citizens
reads: design requirements · the people it is for
returns: the design, the choices the people made, and their reasons
test now: T3 pretest with the same people
test in use: T4 against the control


What the literature says
------------------------

rationale: Involving end users at several stages produces more effective and efficient
  services with higher user satisfaction; the key is an equal relationship with shared
  decisions, which needs power to shift from professionals to users [O'Cathain 2019
  taxonomy].
context: Quality improvement in health and social care, social innovation in public
  services, and radical innovation in health services [O'Cathain 2019 taxonomy]; public
  innovation with citizens [Voorberg 2015].
steps: Six steps: 1. Build a team that includes end users. 2. Share what each brings,
  and understand the problem by observing, interviewing and workshops. 3. Co-create the
  vision by listening to all voices. 4. Co-design the solution with qualitative
  research, rapid ethnography and prototypes. 5. Build the solution. 6. Measure the
  outcomes together [O'Cathain 2019 taxonomy].
strengths: Services have been changed and costs of care reduced this way; it studies what
  people do rather than what they say they do; a detailed guide exists [O'Cathain 2019
  taxonomy].
limitations: Little attention has been paid to the outcomes of co-creation, and the field
  needs quantitative methods because qualitative case studies dominate [Voorberg 2015].
  No study tests it against another method (ours).


Applied to AI
-------------

agent: The agent runs a panel of simulated patients built from real profiles [Argyle
  2023], and asks them to help decide what the design should do, not only to react to a
  draft.
steps: 1. Build the panel from real profiles. 2. Ask each member what the design should do
  for them, and why. 3. Draft options with the panel and let it choose. 4. Record each
  choice and its reason. 5. Bring the chosen design to real patients before use (T3).
returns: The design, the choices the panel made, and their reasons.
verify: A pretest with real patients (T3) before the Exp (T4). Co-design proper needs
  real people with an equal say, which a simulated panel can only prepare for (ours).
risk: A simulated panel is not the people themselves: it cannot share power, and its
  choices are not validated for this population (ours). Generative agents behave
  believably, which is not the same as accurately [Park 2023].
evidence on AI: A language model conditioned on real backstories reproduced the answers of
  human subgroups [Argyle 2023]. No study tests an AI co-designing with simulated users
  yet (ours).

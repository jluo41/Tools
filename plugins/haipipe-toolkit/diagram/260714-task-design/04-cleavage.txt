THE ONE INVARIANT
=================

  The PATTERN owns the shape and the schema. The INSTANCE owns the artifacts and
  the domain specialization. Neither restates the other's job as its own truth.

     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
     │ haipipe-workflow  →  ONE writer of plan-schema.md                     │
     │ fn/stage-*, audit →  READERS of it; writers only of workflow/*.yaml    │
     └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  🔑 Break this and you get two schemas that drift. The pointer in the fn files
     (03 · ①) is what enforces it. Keep the pointer; never fork the schema.


❌ THE ANTI-PATTERN — prose drift around a single-sourced schema
================================================================

```text
   ✅ the SCHEMA is single-sourced   (fn files point at plan-schema.md)
   ❌ the PROSE around it is NOT      (fn files restate ~150 lines of YAML inline
                                       + their own evidence tables + field lists)

   ⇒ single-sourcing the schema does NOT protect the surrounding instructions.
     stage-report.md's "evidence sources" table drifted to manifest.json +
     config_snapshot.yaml while the real results/ contract (run-sh-template.sh,
     metrics-json-schema.md, runtime-yaml-schema.md, authoring-conventions.md §1)
     is metrics.json + runtime.yaml. The schema pointer was correct; the prose
     next to it rotted. This is the H1 defect. See 06.
```

  🚨 THE LESSON: a pointer to a shared schema is necessary, not sufficient. The
     WORDS the instance wraps around that schema are un-sourced and drift first.
